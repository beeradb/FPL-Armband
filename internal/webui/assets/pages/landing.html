What you’re up against

Somebody reads team news for a living. It isn’t you.

The telly We’ve all been there. Saturday, three o’clock, telly on: your captain is on the bench. Again. Everyone in your mini-league knew. Nobody’s job was to tell you.
His dad December 2023: the real update on Erling Haaland’s foot was his own father selling him on FPL. He could have just benched him.
His barber April 2026: Chelsea’s team news appeared to come out of a barbershop — an account said to be Cucurella’s barber, a photo mid-trim, Palmer and João Pedro both out. He tagged the FPL accounts so nobody would miss it. Community-minded.
All three really happened. All three were public.

We don’t know the barber.
We do follow his Twitter.

When it is news, we read it

None of that was a secret.

It was posted, reported, passed round mini-leagues — out there, for anyone who happened to be looking. You weren’t looking, because looking isn’t your job.

When something moves — a doubt in a press conference, a lineup out early, a transfer that won’t be registered in time — we tell the model, with the source and the date. Your numbers change before the deadline, not after it. What we catch, you’ll see — and you’ll see where it came from.

When it is not even news, we model it

And plenty of weeks there is no story to catch at all.

Nobody leaks that one. He’s fourth choice at a club with a Thursday night in Europe. He’s the one who gets rested when it’s 3-0 on the hour. Nothing gets announced, there’s no flag next to his name, and he costs you the week just the same.

That’s the half we model. Every player in your fifteen marked from Nailed down to Fringe, on his card and on the pitch, before you have to decide.

Not dramatic. Weekly.
