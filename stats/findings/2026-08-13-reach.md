## 1. `MinExpectedMinutes` is one-sided, and this is the finding

**Setting `MinExpectedMinutes` to 0 changes nothing** — 0 of 8 cells on every live column — while
raising it to 89 moves everything, 8 of 8 on both metrics. The shipped 55 does no work downward.

⚠️ **Two footnotes added 2026-08-14, neither changing this run's numbers.** (1) The arm is wired at
**−1**, not 0: `SimConfig.MinExpectedMinutes = 0` maps to the shipped 55 and only a negative removes
the floor, so the sentence above should be read as "to no floor". The test does set −1
(`metricreach_test.go:137`); only this prose is loose. (2) **The nesting extension asserted below is
withdrawn** — re-measured on 36 cells the floorless arm moves 2 and `floor=45` moves 1, so the
antecedent "byte-identical at 0" is false off this grid and the generalisation does not carry. This
section's own "**near**-theorem" hedge is what came true. `stats/snapshots/2026-08-14-minfloor/`.

⚠️ **Three corrections to the first version of this section, applied after statistics review. The
null survived; the gloss did not.**

- **"Removing the floor entirely" is wrong.** This removes the *expected-minutes* cliff only.
  `MinMinutes` 600 — the sample-size floor at `simulate.go:1003`, scaled by the data window at
  `squad.go:362` — is unchanged in every arm.
- **"It has nothing to remove that the optimiser wanted" was false as phrased.** The floor cuts
  **96 to 126 players a build**, topped by Salah (£14.1m, 54.1 settled minutes), Saka (£10.0m, 45.4),
  Isak, Palmer and Eze. 55 min/gw is 2090 minutes over 38, which a heavily-substituted starter in a
  good side misses. The first version called them "expensive players who rarely start"; they are not.
- **The mechanism is redundancy with the objective, and that is the better argument** because it
  generalises: `Score` is already minutes-scaled, so the cut population's best score is **2.9 to 3.9**
  against a kept-pool best of **5.6 to 8.1**. The floor deletes players the objective already ranks a
  tier down. The intended claim is the *stronger* one — it removes ~100 real players a build and the
  optimum wants none of them.

**The claim extends to every setting at or below 55 by nesting, not by the two probes.** The removed
set `{Price > £4.5m and settled minutes < f}` is monotone in `f` (`squad.go:456-460`, with
`fodderPrice` fixed at 4.5 because `BenchMinExpectedMinutes` is unset), so the pools nest and an
intermediate floor removes a strict subset. A theorem for an exact optimiser; a **near-theorem** here,
since the DP seed relaxes the club limit and is repaired afterwards.

⚠️ **The effective sample is 8 opening *builds*, not 8 cells over six columns.** `minExp` reaches
three call sites and two are the wildcard and free-hit squads, which never fire because `sweepConfig`
sets no chips; the transfer search is floorless. The knob acts **once per cell**.

**And one thing the 89 arm shows that is not about reachability**: `HOLD` collapses 1842 → 1070 while
`POLICY` barely moves, 2058 → 2012. The floor is a `HOLD`-channel knob whose damage the transfer path
largely repairs.

### On item 3, and ⚠️ a credit claim this file made and has withdrawn

The interaction would have been **identically zero by construction**, not by measurement: the opening
fifteen is byte-identical between 55 and no floor, so the two floor columns are the same squad cell by
cell. Item 3's own pre-check — count how many opening fifteens differ between floor levels — would
have returned 0 and killed the design at the door.

⚠️ **But "the map cost eleven minutes and the 2x2 it invalidated was six arms over 36 cells" is
retro-fitted and is withdrawn.** Item 3 was dropped at `c4447a1`, on the bench-shape main-effect tie;
the map only ran at `1996ee7`, the next commit. The map supplied a **second, independent** reason to
drop something already dropped. The sentence was being used to justify a standing "run the map first"
policy, so the inflation mattered more than usual — that policy should rest on the argument, not on a
payoff another run had already collected.

**The better reason to drop item 3, which this run does support**: its pre-registered mechanism needs
a floor level that makes the eleven *more fragile*. Lowering the floor cannot, by nesting plus the
measured null; raising it makes the eleven **more** nailed, the wrong direction. **No setting of this
knob produces the fragile eleven the hypothesis requires**, so item 3 is `unmeasurable-on-this-harness`
for its own mechanism. That rhymes with the record's existing note that the derived slots express
depth behind a fragile eleven while the replay builds near-nailed ones by construction.

⚠️ Two limits on that: it is measured at 8 of the 36 cells item 3 would have used, and at the
**shipped bench shape only** — nesting does not carry across shapes, since flat and the fixed tuple
change the objective and hence the optimum.

## 2. The theorem/measurement split, computed

The five transfer knobs are **HOLD-invariant in all 8 cells** and move POLICY in all 8.

⚠️ **The split is three theorems and one measurement, not two and two — corrected after review.**
`min_gain` and `FreeCost` are confined to `decide()`, which `HOLD` never calls. **`BankUpTo` belongs
with them**: every consumer is on the `POLICY` path (`simulate.go:1064`, `:1082`, `:1278`, `:1321`,
`:1487`) and none reaches the opening build or `HoldCaptaincyWeekly`, so its HOLD-invariance is as
much a theorem as `min_gain`'s, by a different set of call sites. Reporting any of the three as a
check is reporting that the compiler works.

**`DecisionHorizon` is the one measurement**, and a contingent one: it reaches `HOLD` through
`oracleWindow()` (`minutesoracle.go:171-179`) → `recentIndex` (`:410-432`, gated on
`OracleMinutes`/`OracleLineups`) → `HoldCaptaincyWeekly` (`simulate.go:2589`) whenever such an oracle
is wired, and this run sets none. **The split is per arm, not per knob**, and mis-sorting `BankUpTo`
inside the section making that point is the third time this branch has committed the error it was
documenting.

## 3. `weekly_xi` moves POLICY without changing a single transfer

POLICY 8/8, HOLD 0/8, moves and hits **0/8**.

⚠️ **The framing in the first version was wrong twice.** `cfg.WeeklyXI` has **exactly one** consumer,
`simulate.go:1110`, and it sets `vw.Horizon = 1` on the weekly *fielding* engine only — it never
touches the opening build, the transfer decision, or `HoldCaptaincyWeekly`, which builds its engines
on unmodified `cfg.Weights`. So the HOLD zero **and** the moves/hits zeros are compile-time confined:
theorems by §2's own standard, and §2's rule about not reporting the compiler working was not applied
here. And the stated reason — "`HOLD` re-picks the eleven weekly by definition, so the knob has
nothing to say there" — does not follow: re-picking weekly is not re-picking on a one-week horizon,
which is the only thing `WeeklyXI` changes. Were `HoldCaptaincyWeekly` to read the knob, `HOLD` would
move despite the weekly re-pick.

**The real content is the pairing**: it moves `POLICY` in 8 of 8 cells while changing **no move and no
hit**. That is a measurement and it is the useful half.

## 4. Three other zeros, and what each means

- **`bank_lookahead`** reproduces its recorded byte-identical null, now with a firing condition
  attached ("weeks with a transfer banked") rather than as a bare zero. Consistent with the record's
  "changes nothing and ships off", and still a **simple-effect** null.
- **The two chip-preparation arms are idle, not inert.** This grid places no chip at all — the
  `bench_boost_gw` and `triple_captain_gw` columns are 0 in every cell — so the arms are **untested**
  here. On the full grid the triple captain is placed in 23 of 36 cells.

## 5. A defect in the map, found by the map

The first run printed **eleven** outcome columns and five of them are **not populated by this grid**:
`frozen_points`, `frozen_captain_points` and `weekly_points` are empty strings unless the frozen
rungs are collected, and both chip columns are 0 because no chip is placed. They showed a clean zero
for every arm, which reads exactly as "nothing reaches this metric".

That is the assertion-instead-of-computation failure this map exists to fix, arriving inside the map
— the third time on this branch that an item has committed the error it was written to prevent. The
report now separates unpopulated columns from unreached ones and excludes them from the verdict,
because that distinction is the whole point of the file. The six live columns reproduce byte for byte
across the two runs.

⚠️ **A caveat on all seventeen arms, unflagged in the first version.** This map's baseline sets
`sc.WeeklyXI = true`, which is **not** the sweep default — `runPolicySweep` calls `sweepConfig` with
`false`. Every arm here therefore runs at a non-shipped setting, so every zero is a **simple-effect**
null. For the `MinExpectedMinutes` arm it is harmless, since `WeeklyXI`'s single consumer does not
touch the opening build, but it applies to the table as a whole.

## What is owed

1. **The static half, and it is half a second rather than a sweep.** A purely perturbational map
   reproduces, in its negative cells, the failure it exists to fix. Promote the throwaway probe the
   review used into a committed diagnostic reporting, per cell, the size of the removed set, its top
   player by score, and whether any removed player enters the returned optimum. That converts the
   perturbational null into a static one and re-derives the mechanism instead of asserting it. It
   reuses `loadPairs` / `sweepConfig` / `PointInTimeWith` and needs **no replay**. The eventual
   mechanical form is a **poison value that panics if read**, turning "did not move" into "was not
   read".
2. **A per-cell column for the applied `MinExpectedMinutes`.** `runPolicySweep` deliberately writes
   the applied `BankUpTo` into `cellRow` "so a variant that changes the bank says so in the CSV";
   there is no equivalent for the floor, so **the cells file cannot self-verify this arm** — the
   verification is a code reading. One line.
2. **The oracle column.** `DecisionHorizon`'s `HOLD`-invariance is contingent on `Oracles` being
   empty and this run has none. ⚠️ "Oracles populated" is not one setting — `Oracles` carries `Info`,
   `Decision`, `FeatureScope` and `FeaturesFrom` — so the second column is interpretable only per
   bit, and must be labelled not comparable with any recorded figure.
3. **A grid that places chips**, so the two preparation arms are tested rather than idle.
4. **`runPolicySweep` refusing an arm** reported on a metric its setting has no observed reach into.
   That is the point of the map and it is not wired yet.
