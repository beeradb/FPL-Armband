# The data session: five defects, what each was worth, and what is still owed

**Provenance.** Branch `worktree-prior-half-life-on-repaired-xgc` at **`aa95f75`**, clean tree,
**38 commits** from the merge base `4d61058`. Three reviewers ran over the range and their record
is `reviews/2026-08-13-cae6941/review.md`; two earlier reviews are captured unapplied in
`reviews/2026-08-13-backfill-runs/review.md`. Every figure below is from a run made in this
session or from a committed cells file, and each says which.

**Terms.** `HOLD` is buy-and-hold with weekly re-picking, the metric that decides a scoring
question; `POLICY` adds the weekly transfer decision. A **cell** is one replayed season entered at
one deadline. The **data states** are `shipped` (both expected-goals backfills on), `xgcoff`
(expected-goals repair on, expected-goals-conceded repair off), `xgoff` = `bothoff` (neither).
**Season-clustered** treats each season as one independent unit; **start-fixed** treats the six
entry gameweeks as fixed.

## 1. The evidence base was one reboot from unreproducible

**The defect.** `stats/out/` is gitignored, so every per-sweep variance table had been deleted by a
clean checkout — that much was a known TODO item. What the item did not say, and what turned out to
be true, is that **the cells those tables are computed from were also gone**: nine of fourteen
sweeps survived only in `/tmp`. That is minutes of replay each, in a directory the operating system
is entitled to empty.

**What was done.** Ten cells files committed under `stats/snapshots/2026-08-12-4d61058/cells/`;
`stats/regenerate_mde.sh` rebuilds all fourteen sweeps' tables in seconds, and
`stats/mde_aggregate.py` collects them into `stats/out/mde-all-arms.csv` with a `sweep` column.
**Cells are committed and tables are not** — cells are expensive and immutable, tables are seconds
of R and rot against the script that makes them.

**Practical gain.** The 23 per-arm detection thresholds behind the canonical *median 39 points a
season* are quotable and re-derivable again, so the mechanical form of the
annotate-do-not-re-derive rule is followable. Two exact reproductions fell out and are worth more
than the recovery itself, because neither was aimed at: the minutes half-life arms (231.9 / 80.0 /
89.9 / 133.0 on `POLICY`) and the grid-width control the six-season switch was argued on (12.4 →
8.4 held, 14.4 → 16.1 with transfers). Those establish that the committed cells, the R script and
the recorded figures are one pipeline.

⚠️ **The 23-comparison population was reached by selecting until the count matched**, and review
established that the range then matching is *not* an independent witness: both its endpoints are
the two sweeps already reproduced above, and matching required pooling two estimators after seeing
the target. Read the recovery as "the pipeline reproduces two sweeps exactly, and a population
consistent with the recorded 23 exists" — not as confirmation of the recorded population.

## 2. The expected-goals backfills, measured

**The question.** The six-season grid was adopted partly on "nothing recorded is invalidated",
which rested on the four shipped seasons being byte-identical inside a six-season run. That
establishes grid-invariance only — both sides were today's code — and the backfills repair seasons
inside the shipped four.

**What was done.** 12 replay processes, **2,880 cells** across two runs, cells committed after
every process. Run B on the six-season grid; Run A as a 2×2 on the four-season grid.

**Findings that hold.**

- **The switches nest.** `FPL_NO_XG_REPAIR=1` silently disables the expected-goals-conceded
  reconstruction too, because `applyXGCRepair` sits inside `applyXGRepair` after its early return.
  So the 2×2 has **three live corners**, and the interaction the design was commissioned to
  estimate is zero *by construction rather than by measurement*. It must not be reported as a null.
- **Confinement is exactly 18 of 36 cells**, pre-registered by season and matched exactly; the
  other 18 byte-identical on all ten populated outcome columns.
- **The backfills move 6 of the 24 four-season cells, all 2022-23.** So a recorded level now needs
  its data state named, and reproduction needs the grid switch plus **one** repair switch — not
  two, since one nests the other.

**What does not resolve, and cannot.** The expected-goals-conceded repair reads −0.903 pts/gw
(≈ −34 a season) on `HOLD`, but the effect exists in three seasons, giving two degrees of freedom
and a critical value of 4.303. ⚠️ A season with a native column is one the repair cannot reach, so
widening the six-season grid adds no cluster — **but `scoringPairNames` is a seven-season
`HOLD`-only grid that plays 2019-20, which the repair does reach, for a fourth. Hard, not
impossible, and unrun.**

**Practical gain.** The reproduction recipe is now correct and the confinement is known exactly, so
any figure measured on 2019-20 through 2022-23 can be placed in its data state instead of being
quoted blind.

## 3. The four uncovered sweep blocks, run

**What was done.** `MINW`, `BONUS`, `BENCH`, `MINK` — 19 arms across three data states, **57 arms
and 1,368 cells**, on the four-season grid.

**The structural result is the useful one.** Each block responds in **exactly six cells, all
2022-23**; the other 18 are byte-identical in every data state. So an ordering that "holds in every
corner" is arithmetic over cells that cannot move — and restricted to the six that can, **no
block's ordering survives the data state**. The two blocks whose orderings look most robust,
`MINK` and `BENCH`, are precisely the two whose robustness is supplied by the inert 18.

Nothing resolves: the largest season-clustered |t| across all 57 comparisons is **1.18** against a
critical value of 3.182, and every point estimate moves by less than or barely more than one
standard error of itself.

**Practical gain.** Four recorded comparisons are no longer unchecked against the repaired archive,
and the record now carries a general warning it did not have: a swept ordering measured on
mostly-inert cells is not an ordering anyone measured.

## 4. `MinutesWeight`: a recorded verdict that is not data-state-free

The record used *"1.25 is nominally the worst of five and wins none of 18 decidable cells"* as the
reason "confirmed" was retracted for every projection constant. Across the three data states its
mean rank moves from 4-of-5 to 1-of-5.

⚠️ **That is not an inversion and must not be quoted as one.** Every responding cell is 2022-23, so
three of four season means are identically zero, the season-clustered *t* is **pinned at 1.00 by
construction** — the `DefConCleanCoupling` degeneracy — and the naive |t| runs 0.39 to 1.67. The
movement itself is unmeasured. And **half the recorded sentence survives untouched**: "wins none of
the decidable cells" reads 0, 1 and 2 of 24 across the states, against 4.8 expected by chance from
five arms, so the two criteria point opposite ways in the repaired state.

⚠️ **The repaired arm is not the arbiter either.** The reconstruction prorates straight-line in
minutes with its error concentrated in partial appearances (29.7% MAE against 3.9% for
ever-presents), while `MinutesWeight` is the minutes convexity exponent — so the two are not
independent. Hypothesis, not a measured bias.

**Practical gain.** A sentence that read as evidence *against* a shipped constant has lost its
support, and nothing has replaced it. Carry `MinutesWeight` as unresolved and data-state-dependent.

## 5. The starting elevens were recoverable, and are now recorded

**The defect.** `merged_gw.csv` carries `starts` as zero for all of 2021-22 and 2022-23 through
GW15, and does not carry the column at all before 2022-23. `reconstructStarts` filled that by rank.
Its residual is **systematic, not noise**: a nailed starter withdrawn at half time and a fringe
player eased on at half time both record 45 minutes, they tie, and the tie-break promotes the wrong
one — 24.5% of near-nailed players lose a start against 7.7% gaining one.

**Why nobody had fixed it.** The 2026-08-12 archive audit found the hole but was a column-by-column
pass over the archive's own files, so the only answer available to it was "reconstruct it". *"Can
we recover the truth?"* was never asked — while the answer sat in a payload this repository
**already downloads and discards**: `getPlayerData` carries `position` with `Sub` as an explicit
value, and the expected-goals harvest reads `time` from those same rows.

**What was done.** `stats/understat_starts_backfill.py` reuses that script's cache, crosswalk,
gameweek map and filters. Harvested **36,429 recorded starts across 44,512 player-gameweek rows**
for 2018-19 to 2022-23 GW1-15. (⚠️ An earlier commit message said "44,514 recorded starts"; that
was the row count, not the start count.)

| season | rows | starts |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2018-19 | 10,245 | 8,360 |
| 2019-20 | 10,543 | 8,360 |
| 2020-21 | 9,905 | 8,360 |
| 2021-22 | 9,783 | 8,357 |
| 2022-23 GW1-15 | 4,036 | 2,992 |

**Validation.** On the 22,088 starter slots of the three seasons that record their own starts, the
harvest misclassifies **0.000%**. ⚠️ Scored on those same cells with the same two-sided statistic,
the rank rule scores **11.74%** — the recorded 2.36% is a different statistic (one-sided, doubles
excluded) and the original like-for-like claim was wrong even though it understated the margin.

⚠️ **What the zero validates is the plumbing, not the football.** Both sources descend from the
same official lineup feed, so agreement is expected; what it rules out is a broken crosswalk, a
mis-mapped date, a leaked foreign fixture or a mishandled double. It also cannot see a disagreement
where Understat has a start and the archive has no minutes, because that row is filtered before
comparison. The stronger evidence is about the seasons actually being repaired, and
`club_gameweek_audit` prints it every run: **716/716 club-gameweeks exact in 2020-21, 272/272 in
2022-23's window, 696 exact with 3 short in 2021-22, and zero over-credited anywhere.**

**Two defects found by review and fixed.**

- **The repair was inert.** It ran inside `fetch`, before the season cache is written — the
  placement `season.go` forbids by name. Measured: the cached 2021-22 season held 7,005 starts, all
  rank-reconstructed, **none harvested**. Moved into `repaired()` and pinned by
  `TestTheStartsSwitchWorksOnACacheHit`.
- **A foreign-match leak.** The inherited filter is per gameweek, not per match, so a mid-season
  mover with Premier League minutes *and* a foreign fixture in one window had the foreign match
  counted as a start. Two cells. ⚠️ **The season-total identity could not see it** — 2021-22 was
  wrong in four club-gameweeks by +1, +1, −1, −1 and summed to exactly 8,360, so an equality test
  passed while the harvest was wrong. Replaced by the direction that cannot cancel, plus the
  per-club-gameweek audit above.

⚠️ **Practical gain, stated honestly: at shipped config this changes no replayed cell.** Two
reviewers independently established that nothing in the scoring path reads `Starts` —
`reliabilityFrom` weights `startShare` by exactly zero, and `blankRate` takes the
unified-appearance branch. What the harvest buys is the **lineups and minutes oracles**, which
previously took rank-reconstructed starts as ground truth; the diagnostics; the agent-facing field;
any run under `FPL_NO_UNIFIED_APPEARANCE` or `FPL_RELIABILITY_SPLIT`; and it unlocks the
start/substitute/unused multinomial that `reconstructStarts`' boundary two forbids — which matters
because `OracleLineups` bounds at ≈73 points a season held, the largest of the information family.
**Do not sell this work on a channel that is weighted zero.**

## 6. The standing rule was widened

*"The archive does not have X is unverified until someone greps for X"* was scoped to the archive,
and that scope was the blind spot: a column audit can only ever answer "reconstruct it". It now
covers **every source the pipeline touches** — caches, harvest payloads, captures. A field the code
fetches and throws away is not a field the project lacks.

⚠️ The starting elevens are the **widening**, not an eighth failure of the old rule: the archive
genuinely lacked the column, and that was verified rather than asserted. The count stands at seven.

## 6. The fourth cluster: run, and it does not rescue the measurement

The record called the expected-goals-conceded repair's price unmeasurable because it reaches only
three seasons on the six-season grid — df 2, critical value 4.303. Review refuted the
impossibility: `scoringPairNames` is a seven-season `HOLD`-only grid that also plays **2019-20**,
which has no native column either and which the repair does reach. `HOLD`-only does not bite,
since `HOLD` decides a scoring question. Two arms of `TestDiagBaseline`, 42 cells each, both exit 0.

**The fourth cluster is real**: 24 cells move over four seasons — 2019-20, 2020-21, 2021-22,
2022-23 — and 18 are structurally inert, exactly as the mechanism predicts.

| season | `HOLD` pts/gw | ≈ /season |
|---|---:|---:|
| **2019-20** | **+5.3709** | **+204.1** |
| 2020-21 | −0.2785 | −10.6 |
| 2021-22 | −1.0169 | −38.6 |
| 2022-23 | −1.4150 | −53.8 |

⚠️ **Adding the cluster made the comparison worse, not better, and this is the finding.** 2019-20
comes in at four times any other season and with the opposite sign, so the pooled mean **flips from
−34 to +25 a season**, the season-clustered *t* is **+0.42** on df 3, and the threshold **triples
to 192 a season** because the between-season spread balloons. Only **1 of 4** seasons agrees with
the pooled sign.

**More clusters do not help when the added one is an outlier.** Degrees of freedom entered the
critical value (4.303 → 3.182) and lost badly to the variance the new season brought with it. The
honest reading of the whole exercise is that the repair's price is **unresolved on every grid that
can reach it**, and that the −34 the six-season grid gave was carried by three seasons that happen
to agree rather than by an effect the design can see.

⚠️ **2019-20 is not dismissible as invalid.** Its `POLICY` is excluded from this record — FPL
granted unlimited free transfers before the GW30+ deadline and froze prices for three months — but
its `HOLD` scoring is fine, which is why the grid is `HOLD`-only. What it *is* is the most
reconstructed season on any grid: no native xGC, no native `starts` column, and a three-month
COVID break in the middle. Treat +204 as a season this repair does something large and unmodelled
to, and as a reason to distrust pooling it, rather than as a number to quote.

Cells, provenance and `pair_xgc7.py` are in this directory.

## 7. The six-season default is right for POLICY too, and the record's exception is not supported

**The question.** The six-season grid ships as the default, adopted on a `HOLD` result. On `POLICY`
the same positive control moved the *wrong* way — threshold 14.4 → 16.1 — because 2021-22 arrived
at +1.16 pts/gw against a native range of 0.000 to 0.581 and nearly doubled the between-season
spread. On that single observation the record prescribes `FPL_SWEEP_SEASONS=default` for transfer
settings, and says plainly it is waiting for "more `POLICY` arms with real effects run on both
grids".

**Those arms already existed on one grid.** Five arms on disk clear |t| = 2 on `POLICY`, so only
the six-season half needed running: `MINHL` (5 arms) and `FIXW` (4 arms), both with real `POLICY`
effects and both with four-season cells already committed. 324 cells.

**Clustered standard errors and implied thresholds, four seasons against six:**

| block | arm | `POLICY` thr4 → thr6 | `HOLD` thr4 → thr6 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `MINHL` | flat (no recency) | 191 → **102** | 76 → **48** |
| `MINHL` | half-life 2 | 53 → **73** ⚠️ | 83 → **46** |
| `MINHL` | half-life 8 | 67 → **48** | 48 → **28** |
| `MINHL` | half-life 20 | 130 → **67** | 66 → **35** |
| `FIXW` | fixture weight 0.00 | 67 → **36** | 108 → **57** |
| `FIXW` | fixture weight 0.35 | 38 → **20** | 54 → **29** |
| `FIXW` | fixture weight 1.00 | 36 → **32** | 35 → **20** |

**Widening helps 6 of 7 arms on `POLICY` (median ratio 0.54) and 7 of 7 on `HOLD` (median 0.55,
range 0.52-0.63).** The `HOLD` consistency is the striking part — every arm lands between 0.52 and
0.63, which is close to the √(3/5) the degrees of freedom alone would predict.

**So the vice control's `POLICY` deterioration was that arm meeting that season, not a property of
the grid.** The instruction to sweep transfer settings on the four-season grid is **not supported
by the wider evidence** and should not be followed as a rule.

⚠️ **Three limits, and they stop this being a settled verdict.**

- **Seven arms, two blocks.** Arms within a block share cells and a baseline, so this is nearer two
  independent observations than seven. It refutes "widening always hurts `POLICY`"; it does not
  establish a general ratio.
- **Both blocks are scoring constants, not transfer settings.** The instruction being questioned
  governs *transfer* settings, and that population is still represented only by `hits` at |t| = 2.14
  and the vice control itself. This weakens the exception rather than closing it.
- **`half-life 2` is a real counter-example**, not noise to be waved through: its `POLICY` SE rises
  0.441 → 0.750. Whatever 2021-22 does to the vice control, it does to that arm too. A grid rule
  that is right on average can still be wrong for the arm in front of you — check the season means
  before pooling.

### Closed: block `H` — `min_gain` — settles it on the population the rule governs

The gap above was that both blocks were *scoring* constants while the rule governs *transfer*
settings. `H` is `min_gain`, the transfer gate's minimum-gain threshold, five arms, `Metric:
POLICY` in the block's own header. No four-season cells existed, so both grids were run: 120 and
180 cells.

| arm | `POLICY` thr4 → thr6 | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|
| min_gain = 0.00 | 19 → **12** | 0.80 |
| min_gain = 0.40 | 19 → **12** | 0.80 |
| min_gain = 0.95 | 47 → **25** | 0.66 |
| min_gain = 1.30 | 64 → **40** | 0.77 |

**Four of four help.** Combined with `MINHL` and `FIXW`: **10 of 11 arms**, median threshold ratio
**0.62**. The four-season exception is refuted on its own population, and the guidance is now
"sweep everything on the six-season default".

⚠️ **`MINHL half-life 2` remains the single counter-example** and should not be waved through: its
`POLICY` SE rises 0.441 → 0.750. Whatever 2021-22 does to the vice-captain control it also does to
that arm. The rule is right on average; check season means before pooling any individual arm.

**A free corroboration fell out.** `min_gain = 0.00` and `min_gain = 0.40` have **identical
standard errors to four decimals on both grids** (0.1558 four-season, 0.1250 six-season), which
independently reproduces the recorded finding that those two settings return byte-identical
seasons — the gate's threshold does nothing between 0.0 and 0.4.

⚠️ **`hits` could not be used**, and the reason is worth carrying: the `HITS` block **no longer
exists in the source**. Its rescued cells remain valid evidence of what was measured and cannot be
regenerated. A committed cells file is not proof its sweep still exists.

Reproduce with `grid_policy.py` in this directory.

## 8. The held-out season is the season the repairs changed

**This is the largest open consequence of the session and it was not on anyone's list.**

Several constants are recorded as unvalidated, or as refuted out of sample, because **2022-23
disagreed**. 2022-23 is the held-out season in the bench-slot tables, the reliability mix and the
band-strength test — it was the earliest season the archive reached when those were run.

Run A established that the backfills move **6 of 24 four-season cells, all of them 2022-23**. So
the held-out season is *precisely and only* the season whose data changed. ⚠️ **Every constant
whose verdict rests on 2022-23's out-of-sample behaviour was decided on data that no longer
exists.**

**Measured on 2022-23 alone**, paired against each block's shipped arm on `HOLD`, across the three
data states Task B ran (`heldout_verdicts.py`):

| block | best arm, `shipped` | best arm, `norepair` | max swing pts/gw |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `BENCH` | weight 0.20 | **weight 0.02** | 1.97 |
| `MINW` | exponent 1.50 | **exponent 1.75** | 1.61 |
| `BONUS` | 1.5 / 1.5 | 1.5 / 1.5 | 1.84 |
| `MINK` | `BlendMinutesK` 1 | `BlendMinutesK` 1 | 1.12 |

**The best arm on the held-out season changes with the data state in 4 of 4 blocks** (`BONUS` and
`MINK` change at `xgcoff` rather than at `norepair`). Swings run 0.3 to 2.0 pts/gw — 11 to 76
points a season on that one season.

### What to revisit, in order

1. **The bench slot weights.** The held-out season *disagreeing* is the whole reason the shaped
   tuple is recorded as unvalidated — *"flat is 51 worse on the tuned three and 79 better on the
   held-out season"*. `BENCH`'s 2022-23 winner moves from 0.20 to 0.02 across data states. Two
   tables in `docs/notes/optimiser-and-squad.md` rest on this.
2. **`BandStrength` = 0.** Its out-of-sample refutation is **twelve points on 2022-23 alone**
   (1731 → 1719). Twelve points on the one season that moved is no longer a refutation.
3. **The reliability mix at minutes-share 1.0.** Lower risk — there the held-out season *agreed* —
   but its four-season total of 8304 includes 2022-23's 1719.

### Two things that make it worse, and one that limits it

⚠️ **Those tables are absolute season totals, not paired differences**, which this record's own
convention forbids: *judge a sweep on paired differences, not on totals*. So they are doubly
suspect — the retired method, applied to the season that moved.

⚠️ **A held-out check on native data is no longer possible.** Every season outside the tuned three
(2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26) is a repaired season: 2018-19 through 2022-23 all carry backfilled xG,
xGC or starts. Re-running the held-out check is possible; running it on untouched data is not.

**It does not follow that the constants are wrong.** Most ship on *nothing beats it* rather than on
*this was proven*, so the practical exposure is smaller than the list suggests. What is void is the
**stated justification**, not necessarily the choice. Re-deriving them is a sweep programme, not an
annotation, and it belongs in TODO rather than here.

## What is still owed

the six-season grid, `TestDiagBaseline`, 36 cells each, both exit 0 with the declared arm count —
**byte-identical in all 36 cells on every one of the 10 populated outcome columns**, points and
per-gameweek rates and transfer count and hits alike. Cells and the pairing script are in this

That is worth more than the usual null, for three reasons. It was **pre-registered**: two
reviewers reached it independently by reading the code — `reliabilityFrom` weights `startShare` by

**structural rather than statistical**, so it needs no threshold and cannot be a false negative
from a noisy grid. And it **closes the question the harvest raises**: no recorded figure moves, so
no reproduction recipe needs a starts data state, and the harvest can be merged without
invalidating anything in the record.
