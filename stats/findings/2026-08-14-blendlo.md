## What this closes

The banked `BLEND` run swept 8/12/16/24 with **8 as both the shipped value and the lowest rung**,
so the ladder began at its own baseline and `k=8` had no left-hand neighbour. This adds 3 and 5.

⚠️ **The four shared arms are BYTE-IDENTICAL to the banked run** — 144 cells, `hold_points` and
`policy_points`, **0 differences**. That is two things at once. It is a clean engine-stability check
across `4a56c75` → `5a683b0` (the cells-schema work moved no number). And it means **this run is not
independent evidence about the four arms it shares**: anything quoted for 12/16/24 is the banked
figure re-printed, not a replication in the sense of new data.

## The answer to the question this run was for

**Neither "trough" nor "slope" is established, and the low side is flat.** Read as a ladder in `k`:

    k          3      5      8     12     16     24
    a season  -4.1   +1.8    0    -11.6  +11.6  +12.6

The two arms below 8 carry the **two smallest effects in the ladder**, and they straddle zero.
Whatever structure the ladder has lives at 12 and above.

⚠️ **This is not "the low side is worth nothing".** With per-arm thresholds of 27–48 a season, an
ordinary effect below 8 is entirely unexcluded; the point estimates are near zero and the interval
is wide. What the run does retire is the **asymmetry in the evidence**: before it there was no
measurement below the shipped value at all, so "the optimum is unbounded downward" was a live
reading the design could not address. It is now measured and does not point that way.

**Pre-registered prediction 1 is neither confirmed nor refuted.** It predicted the low arms
non-positive, from the standing finding that an argmax wants more shrinkage than a predictor does.
`k=3` is −4.1 (in direction) and `k=5` is +1.8 (against), both far inside noise. **At this power the
prediction was not testable**, which is a fact about the design rather than about the principle —
and it should have been foreseen when the prediction was written, since the pre-registration's own
prediction 2 says the detectable effect is 39–54.

**Prediction 3 holds: `k=8` ships unchanged.**

**This converts the null from ambiguous into a genuine tie.** The knob reaches the squad in roughly
two-thirds of cells, so "nothing resolves" here means the instrument cannot separate the arms — not
that the comparison failed to run, which is the reading the minutes floor's 2-of-36 forced. Contrast
`MinExpectedMinutes`, where the same check killed a points figure outright.

## ⚠️ One contrast looks significant and is three cells

`k=16 minus k=12` reads **+0.612 pts/gw = +23.3 a season, CR2 t 3.94** against a `t_crit` of 2.571.
It is **not a result**:

- it is positive in **17 of 36 cells** — fewer than half, for a large positive mean;
- **dropping the three largest cells takes it from +23.3 to +0.2 a season.** Three cells of 36 carry
  99% of it;
- it is a raw between-arm p outside the Holm family.

⚠️ **Leave-one-season-out does NOT catch this**, and that is the instructive part: the three cells
are `2021-22@26`, `2022-23@16` and `2024-25@6` — **three different seasons** — so every LOSO subset
keeps two of them and the estimate stays between 19.8 and 28.2. The small CR2 SE (0.156 against a
naive 0.419) has the same cause: season means are consistent while the within-season spread is
enormous, so clustering on season averages the outliers in rather than out. This is the recorded
"clustering is not always conservative" arriving from an unfamiliar direction, and it means **LOSO
is not sufficient on its own** — a leave-one-*cell*-out or a positive-cell count is needed beside it.

## The schedule test — stronger, still not resolving, and NOT independent

`d(slope)/d(entry gw) = 0.0091, t 2.21, p 0.078, df 5`; swing **180.9** a season against this
ladder's own p=0.05 threshold of **210.3** and an 80%-power MDE of 285.5.

Against the banked four-arm figure (0.0076, t 1.28, p 0.257, swing 115.2, threshold 231.3) this is
the same sign and a larger t. ⚠️ **Do not read that as corroboration.** The shared arms are
byte-identical, so the movement from t 1.28 to 2.21 is the effect of **adding two rungs to the same
cells**, not of new evidence. And the schedule test was pre-registered for `BLEND`, not for this
run, so here it is **exploratory**. It still does not clear 0.05 and its swing is still below its
own threshold.

This is also **the first ladder whose slope is licensed by the design rather than by arm labels** —
the arms declare their setting through a getter run on the applied config. `schedule_screen.R`
prints which of the two licensed the slope.

⚠️ **A bug found by running this**: the screen's first version plumbed the declared settings into
one branch of the block list and not the other, so it fell silently back to label parsing **and
printed "this bank predates the `setting` column"** — a false statement about the data, produced by
the guard written to make provenance honest. `ladder_of`'s `settings` argument is now required with
no default, so a call site that forgets is an error rather than a silent downgrade. Absence must be
a property of the file, never of the caller.

## What ships

**Nothing.** `k=8` stays, on the same footing the banked run left it: the out-of-sample calibration,
and the practice of not moving an unresolved constant. This run does not strengthen that footing —
it adds two rungs that say nothing — but it removes one specific way the old evidence was
incomplete.
