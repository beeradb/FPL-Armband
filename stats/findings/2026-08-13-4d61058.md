# The two expected-goals backfills, measured — 2026-08-13

Two measurement runs at `4d61058`. No model change, no constant moved, nothing shipped.
Everything here is a paired difference between two **data states**, produced by separate replay
processes, because `FPL_NO_XG_REPAIR`, `FPL_NO_XG_AGGREGATE` and `FPL_NO_XGC_REPAIR` all act
during `Load` and `runPolicySweep` calls `loadPairs` once, before the variant loop, into a
process-global season cache. An arm setting one of them inside its `apply` replays the same
already-parsed season in both arms and reports a clean tight null on exactly the thing it was
built to measure.

**The predictions were written down first**, in `2026-08-13-4d61058-PREREGISTRATION.md`, committed at `f6b43c7`
before a single cell was computed. They are P1 to P8 below and each is marked held or failed.

**This document has been through `fpl-stats-review` and eleven of its findings are applied in
place.** Three of them changed a claim rather than softening it: a sentence about the per-season
cell splits was factually wrong about its own data, the season-clustered *t* is the least
trustworthy of the three available rather than the most, and the sign of the headline **flips
across defensible estimands**. Where the review corrected something, the correction is marked
⚠️ rather than silently applied.

**Provenance, stated as the artefacts actually read it.** Every process ran at the same commit
with the same source tree; all sidecars stamp `dirty=true`, and the dirt is the untracked cells
directory the runs were writing into — `snapshot.GitState` uses `git status --porcelain`, which
counts untracked files. The sharper statement is the pairwise one: `runB-xgc-on.provenance.csv`
and `runB-xgc-off.provenance.csv` are **identical across all 193 key/value rows** except
`constants_digest` and one added row, `env  FPL_NO_XGC_REPAIR  1`. Since the fingerprint folds
env switches into the digest, the digest difference *is* the switch. That is a stronger record
than the 2026-08-11 pair, which stamped `dirty=true` on one arm and `false` on the other.

## 0. Two switch couplings, found by reading and then measured

These cost two seconds each and they change what everything below can mean.

### `FPL_NO_XG_REPAIR=1` silently disables the xGC reconstruction as well

`applyXGRepair` returns early when `noXGRepair()` is true (`xgrepair.go:283-287`), and the call
to `applyXGCRepair` sits **inside** `applyXGRepair`, after that early return (`:366-369`).
Measured on the parsed seasons, `TestDiagXGCPoints`' coverage table:

| played season | appearances with xGC, off then on, **shipped** | the same under `FPL_NO_XG_REPAIR=1` |
|---|---|---|
| 2019-20 | 0 to 10,543 | 0 to **0** |
| 2020-21 | 0 to 9,903 | 0 to **0** |
| 2021-22 | 0 to 9,779 | 0 to **0** |
| 2022-23 | 6,496 to 10,529 | 6,496 to **6,496** |
| 2023-24 / 2024-25 / 2025-26 | unchanged | unchanged |

The diagnostic's own guard fires — *"no played season's xGC coverage moved, so this measured
nothing"* — which is the check working.

**P1 held, and on its own pre-registered test rather than only upstream of it.** The
pre-registration defined P1 as the `xgoff` and `bothoff` cells files being identical in every
cell. Run A supplies that: **192 cells a side across 8 arms, zero differing**, and the two
`.means.csv` files agree to all 17 significant digits on every arm and every metric.

**So the 2x2 has three distinct corners, not four.** `xgoff` **is** `bothoff`, and the
interaction term the design was commissioned to estimate is **zero by construction rather than
by measurement**. It must not be reported as a null: a null is a measurement that came out
small, and this is a cell the switches cannot reach.

What remains estimable is a decomposition, and it is worth having:

| contrast | what it isolates |
|---|---|
| `shipped` − `xgcoff` | the **xGC reconstruction**, alone |
| `xgcoff` − `bothoff` | the **xG/xA backfill**, *conditional on xGC being off* |
| `shipped` − `aggoff` | the **season-aggregate** half of both backfills |

Those three are a **sequential** decomposition and they are exactly additive by construction —
the first two sum to the third — so nothing is being double-counted. What is *not* available is
the interaction: the xG effect is measured only with xGC off, and the xGC effect only with xG on,
and the fourth corner that would let those two conditionals be compared does not exist. Separating
it needs a code change — moving the `applyXGCRepair` call out of `applyXGRepair`, or giving it its
own gate first. Left undone deliberately: this was commissioned as measurement.

### `FPL_NO_XG_AGGREGATE=1` governs the expected-goals-**conceded** aggregate too

`xgcrepair.go:437` gates the xGC season-aggregate rebuild on
`spec.NoAggregate && !noXGAggregate()` — the same `noXGAggregate()` the xG side reads. Measured,
`TestDiagXGCCoverage`'s "season totals rebuilt" column:

| season | shipped | `FPL_NO_XG_AGGREGATE=1` |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2018-19 | 505 | **0** |
| 2019-20 | 515 | **0** |
| 2020-21 | 524 | **0** |
| 2021-22 | 537 | **0** |

The switch named for expected goals turns off both backfills' aggregate halves. That is not a
defect — one switch for "leave the season totals alone" is a reasonable design — but the **name**
says otherwise, and this record's most expensive failures are all labels that stopped describing
what they name.

The channel matters and it is clean: `newPriorIndexMulti`'s `stat` closure reads season
**aggregates** (`q.XG`, `q.XA`, `q.XGC`), never weekly rows, at the shipped `PriorRateHalfLife`
of 0. So the aggregate half acts purely as **the prior** and the weekly half purely as **the
played season**.

## 1. Run B — what the xGC reconstruction is worth, on the six-season grid

**Design.** Two processes, `TestDiagBaseline` (one arm, shipped settings, nothing varied), the
six-season grid (`FPL_SWEEP_SEASONS` unset), 36 cells each, exit 0 both times. Paired afterwards
on (season, start gameweek). Because the arm is the baseline itself, the paired difference **is**
what the repair is worth in points.

The grid is six, not four. `TODO.md`'s entry contradicted itself — title "the full six-season
grid", body "18 of 36 cells", and one sentence saying `FPL_SWEEP_SEASONS=default`, which is the
*four*-season grid where 18 of 36 is not arithmetically possible. Fixed in this commit.

### The confinement is exactly 18 of 36 — P5 held, in both directions

| played season | cells that differ | cells byte-identical |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2020-21 | **6** | 0 |
| 2021-22 | **6** | 0 |
| 2022-23 | **6** | 0 |
| 2023-24 | 0 | **6** |
| 2024-25 | 0 | **6** |
| 2025-26 | 0 | **6** |

Every affected cell moved and no unaffected cell did, on all ten populated outcome columns —
points, per-gameweek rates, transfer count and hits. (`frozen_*` and `weekly_*` are blank
throughout this sweep and are skipped rather than compared.)

**This block does two jobs and the write-up previously claimed only one.** It is the confinement
check, about the repair. It is *also* a **process-equivalence control**, about the design: two
processes, two parses, two optimiser runs, byte-identical in 18 of 18 cells. Together with
`TestSeedOrderIsDeterministic` that is what licenses pairing cells across processes at all.
(`xgcpoints_test.go:23`'s remark that "`Optimize` is separately not run-to-run deterministic" is
**stale** — the fix is in this tree.)

Note what the eighteen zeroes are *not*. 2023-24's prior is 2022-23, which gains 4,033
reconstructed weekly rows — and 2023-24 still reads exactly zero, because `rebuildXGCAggregates`
correctly declines to touch 2022-23's already-complete season total and a prior is read only
through that total. The zero row that looks least informative is the one that tests confinement
rather than the escape hatch.

### Why the 18, and not the 36

Two reasons, and the second is better than the one first written here.

1. **Dilution.** The other 18 are guaranteed zeroes; pooling them halves the estimate and reports
   the dilution as a smaller effect. The halving is exact and checkable in the artefacts:
   −0.9034 / 2 = −0.4517, which is the 36-cell pooled figure to four decimals. The 18 cells are
   balanced 3 × 6 across entry points, so the restriction does not disturb the per-gameweek
   weighting.
2. ⚠️ **It fabricates degrees of freedom, which is worse.** On all 36 cells the season-clustered
   *df* reads **5**, because three season means are exactly 0.000. Those are not three draws of a
   season effect; they are three constants. The df-2 position is honest and the df-5 one is not.

**This is not selection on the outcome.** The set was named by season in the pre-registration,
from a mechanism argument, before the run; the observed moved-set matched it exactly.

For completeness, since a reader is otherwise told that pooling 36 is wrong and never told what
it gives: over all 36 cells `HOLD` reads −0.452 pts/gw (−17.2 a season, p = 0.132) against
−0.903 (−34.3, p = 0.113) over 18. **The restriction doubles the headline and moves the evidence
essentially not at all** — so it is a choice about the estimand, not about power.

### The number, and the three estimators — none of which resolves it

| estimator | `HOLD` pts/gw | *t* | df | threshold, pts/season |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| naive, all cells independent | −0.903 | −1.21 | 17 | ~59 |
| **start fixed, no season effect** | −0.903 | **−1.02** | 10 | **75.2** |
| season-clustered (CR2) | −0.903 | −2.71 | 2 | 54.4 |

−0.903 pts/gw is about **−34 points a season**. `POLICY` reads +0.511 (+19.4 a season, CR2
t = +0.74) and is reported for completeness only — this is a scoring change, so `HOLD` decides.

⚠️ **The clustered *t* is the least trustworthy of the three, not the most, and an earlier
version of this section had the reason backwards.** The three season means (−0.279, −1.017,
−1.415) have sd 0.577 where within-season noise alone predicts 1.538. `variance_components.txt`
reports this as `F_seas = 0.14, p = 0.871` — an F *below one*. The season means landed **closer
together than chance alone predicts**, which is a low chi-square draw on 2 df, not seasons
agreeing about a real effect. It makes the clustered SE anticonservatively small. The harness's
own printed rule — *"prefer `t fixd` where `%seas` is near zero"* — points at −1.02 on df 10,
since `%seas` is exactly 0.0 here, and `variance_components.txt` says outright: *report the
range, do not pick an end.*

⚠️ **The threshold is the *t* restated, not a second witness.** `sig_season = t_crit × SE × 38`,
so "−34 against 54" is arithmetically identical to "|t| = 2.71 < 4.303". Quote the range —
**roughly 55 to 150 a season** depending on estimator (54 empirical CR2 at df 2, 75 start-fixed,
145 model-based with the season component floored at zero) — and note that a threshold estimated
from a 2-df variance is itself a noisy estimate of noise: the one-sided bound on `sd_season` is
[0.00, 2.03]. The record's canonical median of 39 is a *four-season* figure across 23 arms and
is not a benchmark for a three-effective-season comparison; the comparison is worse than typical,
which is the point.

**Unresolved on every estimator, and "unresolved" is not "refuted".**

⚠️ **The inferential position is worse than anything in the canonical block, and widening the
grid cannot help.** The effect exists in three seasons, so df 2 and a critical value of 4.303. The
two seasons the six-season widening added are themselves two of the three carrying the effect,
and 2023-24 onward can never contribute because they have a real xGC column — **a season with
native xGC is a season where the repair is inert.** A Rademacher wild cluster bootstrap floors at
p = 0.250 on three clusters.

⚠️ **And the external-validity confound is severe and unbreakable.** The three reachable seasons
are, by construction, the three worst-instrumented in the archive: all three carry `starts`
reconstructed by rank, all three carry the borrowed Understat provider offset, and 2020-21 was
played behind closed doors. The estimand is "the xGC repair, conditional on reconstructed starts
and backfilled xG, on two COVID-affected seasons and two thirds of a third." **A held-out season
is structurally unavailable**, so one of the three shapes this project accepts as evidence is off
the table by construction.

### The point estimate is negative on every estimand but one, and none of them resolves

⚠️ **Headed "the sign flips across defensible estimands, which is the decisive fact" until
2026-08-13.** Three of the four estimands are negative; the one positive is a **three-cell**
GW1-only subset whose own |t| is **0.17**, and the fourth is the all-36-cell reading this document
forbids sixteen lines earlier. Read the table as *nothing here is distinguishable from zero*
rather than as a sign flip.

| estimand | `HOLD`, pts/season |
|---|---:|
| equal-weighted per-gameweek × 38 (quoted above) | **−34.3** |
| inverse-variance weighted — the efficient estimator of a full season from GW1 | −24.5 (p = 0.451) |
| **GW1 entries only — the only cells that are a whole season** | **+13.6** |
| all 36 cells, equal-weighted | −17.2 |

`variance_components.txt` makes the case for the second itself, and prints the axis that explains
the third:

| `HOLD` pts/gw by entry gameweek | GW1 | GW6 | GW11 | GW16 | GW21 | GW26 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| | **+0.36** | −0.76 | +0.73 | **−2.48** | −1.63 | −1.64 |

Early entries (GW1/6/11) average **+0.11**; late entries (GW16/21/26) average **−1.92**.

⚠️ **Called "a two-point-per-gameweek gradient" and "stronger than the season sign count" here;
both are withdrawn, 2026-08-13.** It is not a gradient — GW11 sits above GW6, so the six are not
monotone — and it is a two-group contrast found after seeing the data, three cells per column,
with no standard error. Measured properly the early-minus-late contrast is **+2.03 pts/gw at
naive t = 1.40 and season-clustered t = 0.91 on df 2**, against a critical value of 4.303, and it
**reverses in one of the three seasons** (+2.60 / +5.56 / −2.08). GW1's own column mean is +0.36
at **t = 0.17**. So it is *weaker* than the season sign count this document grades at p = 0.25,
not stronger — and it is **not** grounds to retract the "one cell is not a reading" caution,
which stands.

### What the sign is, and what it is not evidence for

⚠️ **P7 pre-registered the sign of the points effect as NOT predicted, and that stands.** Three
readings are live and the write-up commits to none:

1. **The sign is a draw.** −0.903 pts/gw is one to three standard errors depending on estimator,
   it is the size of the squad-flip sensitivity this harness is known for, and it reverses under
   two of four defensible estimands. This is the best-supported reading and it was missing from
   the first version of this document.
2. **A better-specified objective making a worse policy** — this record's most repeated finding,
   five instances. **A hypothesis, and not tested by this run.**
3. **The reconstruction's own known error.** It over-credits a substituted starter by 4-8% per
   appearance and under-credits a late substitute heavily (`xgcrepair.go`'s minutes table). A
   defender-heavy squad chosen on a biased xGC is not a squad chosen on a better-specified
   objective. **What would separate 2 from 3** is re-running with the non-linear proration
   `xgcrepair.go` already measures and does not apply, and seeing whether the sign survives.
   Unrun.

⚠️ **What this retires is only the description of the repair as a *free* improvement in
specification.** An earlier version said "the one thing that is established is that this is not
free" and asserted a measured price. That over-claims: `established` is this project's strongest
verdict word and no estimator here earns it (|t| 1.02 to 2.71, every p ≥ 0.11), and the sign is
not stable across estimands. The defensible statement is that the point estimate is negative at
about the size of the largest constants anyone argues over, the interval spans zero comfortably,
and the assumption that the repair costs nothing is no longer available.

`TestDiagXGCPoints`' recorded per-cell table reproduces **exactly**: `HOLD` +59 / +68 / +118 /

### One check that is unavailable

`sweep_inference.R` prints *"no `.means.csv` beside the cells file, so Go's means cannot be
checked. This is the pipeline's only end-to-end test."* That is unavoidable for a cross-process
pairing, and `pair_cells.py` deliberately refuses to hand-write one — supplying the check with
its own answer would turn a pipeline test into a tautology. The consequence is that these means
are cross-checked only by a second script by the same author, not by Go.

### The control that is missing

There is no near-null arm on these three seasons — a `TestDiagNoiseFloor`-style 1-2% nudge
restricted to 2020-21, 2021-22 and 2022-23 — so the squad-flip sensitivity that reading 1 above
appeals to is asserted from the record's general figure rather than measured on this population.
Cheap, unrun, and named here rather than left implicit.

## 2. Run A — the 2x2 on the four-season grid, and whether recorded figures reproduce

**Design.** `FPL_SWEEP_SEASONS=default` (24 cells). Five corners, five separate processes each:
`TestDiagBaseline` (1 arm) + `TestDiagProjection` at `EXP=MINHL` (5) + `TestDiagViceCaptainFix`
(2) = 8 arms and 192 cells apiece; then a second wave of `TestDiagProjection` at `EXP=FIXW` (4
arms, 96 cells). All exit 0 with the declared arm count.

**Scope, stated plainly rather than narrowed silently.** The first launch asked for all seven
`TestDiagProjection` blocks in every corner — 175 arms — and it was cut to two blocks.

⚠️ **The throughput figure this cut was justified on was wrong, and it is why the run was
truncated. Corrected 2026-08-13 from these runs' own logs.** The sentence here read *"about one
24-cell arm per 96 seconds in aggregate (five concurrent sweeps return the same total throughput
as one), so that is four and a half hours"*, and the parenthetical appears nowhere in the logs.
Measured: `MINHL` ran 8 arms in 16:08-16:54 per process — **~127 s/arm per process, ~25 s/arm
aggregate over five concurrent** — and `FIXW` 4 arms in ~9:30. Run B at two concurrent ran 5.6
s/cell against Run A's 5.3 s/cell at five, so **per-process rate did not degrade and concurrency
scaled**. The real cost of the full ask was closer to **1.2 hours** than four and a half. The four
uncovered blocks were subsequently run in one wave — see §4 — which is the practical
demonstration. **A wrong cost estimate truncated a run; record the measured rate, not a
remembered one.**

- **`MINHL`, the minutes half-life ladder.** The most-cited constant table in the record, on
  `HOLD`, so there is a recorded figure to check reproduction against, and its mechanism acts in
  every cell.
- **`FIXW`, the fixture weight ladder.** Chosen on *mechanism*: `fixture_weight` scales the
  attacking and defensive multipliers, which multiply exactly the quantities the two backfills
  repair.
- **`DCC` was the thematically obvious pick and is provably useless here.** Defensive
  contribution scores in **2025-26 alone**; on the four-season grid the backfills reach
  **2022-23 alone**. The live sets are disjoint, so a `DCC`-by-data-state interaction is
  identically zero by construction. Avoided by arithmetic rather than by a run.
- **Not covered at the time:** `MINW`, `BONUS`, `BENCH`, `MINK`. Each is a recorded comparison and
  none was run under the data states. **A scope cut, not a null.** ✅ **All four have since been
  run — see §4.** `DCC` is deliberately absent from this list: it is closed on mechanism above,
  not uncovered, and listing it beside genuinely unrun blocks invited someone to run it.

1. **The paired means reproduce across two days and a code change** — on all four rungs, both

## 4. Task B — the four uncovered blocks, run

19 arms per data state across `MINW` (minutes convexity exponent, ships 1.25), `BONUS` (bonus
prior/evidence schedule, ships 0.5/1.5), `BENCH` (opening bench weight, ships 0.10) and `MINK`
(minutes prior strength, ships 5), on `FPL_SWEEP_SEASONS=default`. Three data states — `shipped`
(both backfills on), `xgcoff` (expected-goals repair on, expected-goals-conceded repair off) and
`norepair` (`FPL_NO_XG_REPAIR=1`, which by the nesting in §0 turns **both** off, and is therefore

Five corners were not run and should not be: `xgoff` **is** `bothoff` by construction, so the

Reproduce with `taskb_analysis.py` in this directory.

### The confinement holds again: 6 of 24, all 2022-23, in every block

Each of the four blocks responds in **exactly six cells, all of them 2022-23** — the same
confinement P2 pre-registered and Run A found. The other 18 cells are byte-identical across all
three data states in all four blocks.

⚠️ **This is why an ordering that "holds in all three states" must not be counted as three
confirmations.** Three quarters of the grid is the same numbers in every state, so a stable
ordering over all 24 cells is arithmetic. Every ordering below is therefore reported twice: over
all cells, and over the six that can actually move.

Across all 57 comparisons the largest season-clustered |t| is **1.18**, against a critical value
of **3.182** at three degrees of freedom. Every entry in every ladder is unresolved, which is the

### The result that matters: `MinutesWeight`'s recorded ordering does not survive the data state, and neither state can arbitrate

The record says shipped 1.25 *"is nominally the **worst** of five and wins none of 18 decidable
cells"*, and uses it as the reason the word "confirmed" was retracted for the projection
constants. Re-run across the three data states:

| data state | rank of shipped 1.25 | best alternative | cells it wins outright |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `norepair` — the archive the verdict was recorded on | **4 of 5** | +0.250 pts/gw (1.15) | **0 of 24** |
| `xgcoff` | **1 of 5** | −0.043 (1.15) | 1 of 24 |
| `shipped` — both backfills, today | **1 of 5** | −0.058 (1.15) | 2 of 23 |

**The direction reproduces on the unrepaired archive and reverses on the repaired one.** With the
backfills off, three of four alternatives beat the shipped exponent and it wins nothing, which is
the recorded finding. With them on, **every alternative is negative and 1.25 is nominally best of
five.**

Two things this is not. It does **not** resolve — the largest |t| in the block is 1.09, so neither
reading is an effect, and 1.25 is not hereby established. And "wins none of **18** decidable cells"
does not reproduce exactly: this grid has 23-24 decidable cells, so the reproduction is
**directional**, on a model several commits older.

What it does do is withdraw the support for a recorded sentence that reads as evidence *against* a
shipped constant. ⚠️ **Read it as the general warning rather than as a fact about
`MinutesWeight`: a nominal ordering measured on the unrepaired archive can invert under the
repair, and four such orderings were carried in this record as if they were data-state-free.**

### ⚠️ And the repaired state is NOT the arbiter here — the repair is non-independent of this constant

An earlier version of this section called the recorded verdict "an artifact of the unrepaired
archive", which asserts the repaired reading is the true one. **That is not established, and for
this particular constant there is a specific reason to doubt it.**

- **The reconstruction is linear in minutes by construction.** `xgcrepair.go:378` distributes a
  club's expected goals conceded as `share := Minutes / (90 * n)` — a straight line through the
  origin.
- **Its validated error is concentrated exactly there.** Against the four seasons that carry a
  real column: ever-presents ratio **1.0088 at 3.9% MAE**, partial appearances **0.9853 at 29.7%
  MAE**. The file's own comment attributes the direction to "what linear minutes-prorating
  predicts against a late-loaded" goal distribution — so the residual is a *minutes-shape*
  residual, not noise.
- **`MinutesWeight` is the minutes convexity exponent.** Choosing the curvature of a
  minutes response using data whose defensive component was distributed straight-line in minutes
  is not an independent measurement of that curvature.
- **All six responding cells are 2022-23**, whose GW7 is absent outright and whose GW1-15 carries
  no native expected goals either, so the season carrying this contrast is the most reconstructed
  one on the grid.

⚠️ **A fourth bullet here overstated the `starts` channel and is withdrawn.** It said 2022-23's
rank-reconstructed `starts` bear on this "because that is precisely the population a minutes
exponent is about". **`starts` does not reach `MinutesWeight` at all**: `reliabilityFrom`
(`metrics.go:1711`) computes `w*minutesShare + (1-w)*startShare` with
`w = reliabilityMinutesShare = 1.0` (`sweep.go:122`), so the start-share term is multiplied by
exactly zero, and `MinutesWeight` is an exponent on minutes alone. That is the shipped consequence
of "reliability should be minutes, not minutes mixed with starts". Starts reach squad selection
only indirectly, through `blankRate` and the derived bench-slot weights — a real channel, but not
the one that was claimed. **The linear-proration argument above is unaffected and is the load-bearing
one.**

⚠️ **This is a mechanism concern, not a measured bias.** Nothing here shows the repaired arm is
wrong, and the reconstruction validates well in aggregate (pooled ratio 0.9994 over 38,973
player-gameweeks). What it shows is that the repaired and unrepaired states are **not** a clean
"wrong data versus right data" contrast for *this* constant, so the inversion cannot be resolved by
preferring the repaired arm.

**The honest verdict is therefore weaker and more useful than either arm.** `MinutesWeight` is
**unresolved and now known to be data-state-dependent**, with no state entitled to arbitrate. The
test that would break the tie is one that does not read the reconstruction's minutes assumption at
all — the substitution-corrected reconstruction already queued in §3, or restricting the contrast
to ever-presents, where the reconstruction's own MAE is 3.9% rather than 29.7%. Both are unrun.

### The other three blocks: orderings survive on paper and not where they can move

| block | ordering over all 24 cells | over the 6 responding cells |
|---|---|---|
| `MINK` | **identical in all three states** (3 > 1 > 8 > 12) | **differs in all three** |
| `BENCH` | **identical in all three states** (0.05 > 0.02 > 0.20) | differs; and it **inverts** — 0.20 is worst overall and best in `shipped`/`xgcoff` |
| `BONUS` | differs across states | differs across states |
| `MINW` | top arm stable (1.15), rest reorders | differs in all three |

So the two blocks whose orderings look robust are exactly the two whose robustness is supplied by
the 18 cells that cannot respond. **On the cells that can, no block's ordering survives the data
state.** That is a bound on how much any of these swept orderings is worth, and it is the same
lesson as the argmax rule this record already carries: an ordering over mostly-inert cells is not
an ordering anyone measured.

### What is now closed and what is not

**Closed:** the four blocks are run and the scope cut is discharged. The throughput figure that
caused the cut is corrected in §2 against these runs' own logs.

**Not closed:** none of the 57 comparisons resolves, and none can on this grid — the responding
cells are one season, so there is no season axis inside the part that moves. The `MinutesWeight`
reversal is a nominal one and wants re-checking on the six-season grid, where 2020-21 and 2021-22
also respond and the contrast gains a season axis. That is the same two-arm-per-state design as
Task A and is the obvious next run.
