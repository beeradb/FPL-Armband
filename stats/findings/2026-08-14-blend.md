# `BlendRateK`: the cells are banked, the pre-registered sign held, and nothing resolves

**Run 2026-08-14** at `4a56c75`, `EXP=BLEND scripts/replay -run TestDiagRejudge -v -timeout 3h`.
4 arms × 36 cells on the six-season grid (2020-21 … 2025-26) at entry gameweeks 1/6/11/16/21/26.
**144 cells**, in `cells/blend.csv`. Elapsed 10m49s, peak RSS 111 MB. Predictions in
`2026-08-14-blend-PREREGISTRATION.md`, written and committed before the run.

`sweep_inference.R` reproduces Go's paired means on all 12 comparisons, **max abs diff 0**.

## What this closes

The schedule screen named `BlendRateK` its strongest untried candidate and **could not reach it**:
the `want("BLEND")` block had existed since it was written and was never run to the archive. That
gap is closed. It was the third instance of "a constant having been *swept* does not mean its cells
were *banked*", after `BandStrength`.

## The points ladder: non-monotone, and nothing resolves

| `BlendRateK=12` | −0.306 | **−11.6** | −0.67 | 5 | 0.533 | 1.000 | 4 of 6 |

**Down at 12, up at 16 and 24** — not a ladder, a rough surface, which is what this record expects
of a projection constant re-tuned at this scale. The block's own recorded prediction was that
raising `k` should help; 16 and 24 are favourable in *direction* and 12 is not, and none of the
three is separable from zero. `BlendRateK=24` has **3 of 6 seasons agreeing**, which is a coin flip.

**Nothing ships changed.** 8 stays, on the record's practice that an unresolved comparison does not
move a constant. ⚠️ **But do not lean on the non-monotonicity to justify it** — with CR2 SEs of
0.40-0.55 pts/gw the whole ladder is consistent with flat, and "12 is worse than both its
neighbours" is exactly the jitter-sized feature this record warns is manufactured by chance.
⚠️ **And `k=8`'s stated support has quietly changed.** It was "two independent methods": an
out-of-sample calibration (MAE on xG/90, 218 players) **and a 24-cell replay** — and the replay is
the table this run reverses. After this, `k=8` stands on the calibration and on not moving an
unresolved constant. That is a weaker position than the record asserts, and the direction of the
new (unresolved) evidence is *upward*.

## The schedule test: the pre-registered sign held, at half its own MDE

> **Pre-registered:** `d(slope)/d(entry gw) > 0`, because `k`'s leverage is monotone in `n90` — at
> `n90 = 0` the current season carries zero weight whatever `k` is — so a late entrant spends its
> whole scored window where `k` does the most work.

**Observed: +0.0076, t = 1.28, p = 0.257 on 5 df.** Positive, as registered — **and that is the
whole result.**

⚠️ **"At half its own MDE" is not a second fact, and an earlier draft of this document quoted it as
though it were.** `swing / threshold` is **identically** `t / t_crit`: the entry span, the setting
range and the 38 all cancel. 115.2 / 231.3 = 0.498 and 1.28 / 2.571 = 0.498. So the two numbers are
one number in two costumes. Report them for the **scale** they put the effect on, never as
corroboration of each other.

⚠️ **And "MDE" was the wrong word**, in this document, in the note and in the script's own column
header. 231.3 is the **p = 0.05 detection threshold**; the 80%-power MDE on
`stats/variance_components.R`'s convention — `(t_crit + qt(0.8, df)) × SE` — is **314**. The script
now prints both as `thr05` and `mde80`.

⚠️ **"The largest swing of any ladder screened" is an argmax over seven, and the scale is not
neutral.** `swing = estimate × entry-span × the ladder's own setting range × 38`, and BLEND's arms
span 8→24, the widest range in the bank — the same arm design this document calls a defect two
sections down. On the scale-free ratio (which is `t / t_crit`) **BLEND is second, 0.498 against
`FIXW`'s 0.516.** Quote it as the largest *raw swing*, with both caveats, or not at all.

**As registered before the run: this null is a tie, and specifically not evidence that `BlendRateK`
is flat across entry points.**

The per-entry columns, `HOLD` pts/gw, paired against `k=8`:

| arm | GW1 | GW6 | GW11 | GW16 | GW21 | GW26 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

Two things are consistent across all three arms, which is what makes them worth reporting at all
given nothing resolves:

- **GW1 is near zero and perfectly ordered in `k`** (+0.035 / +0.092 / +0.158), with **7 of its 18
  arm-cells exactly zero**, the most of any column. `k` cannot touch the *opening fifteen* at GW1, so
  the column is the weekly re-picks over the following 38 weeks.

  ⚠️ **The reason is an early return, not `n90 = 0`, and a first draft had this wrong in four
  places.** `blendFor` returns from its `played == 0` branch (`internal/analysis/blend.go:297`)
  **before** `rk := e.Weights.BlendRateK` is read at `:359`. It is *not* that the blend weight goes
  to zero: pre-season the element carries **last season's** minutes
  (`internal/backtest/replay.go:82` sets `el.Minutes, el.Starts = q.Minutes, q.Starts`), so `n90` is
  ~25-30 for an ever-present — which is CLAUDE.md's own standing rule about FPL's aggregates. **GW1
  is a separate code path, not the limit of the same curve.** The in-season leverage argument, which
  is what the prediction rested on, is unaffected.
- **GW26 is the largest column and monotone in `k`** (+1.692 / +2.987 / +3.500).

## Three caveats — and they are ONE fact seen three ways, which is worse

⚠️ **A first draft called these "three caveats, any one sufficient", which reads as three independent
problems.** They are not independent. The five largest GW26 arm-cells are **2021-22 (+11.31, +9.62)
and 2023-24 (+9.54, +9.31, +6.46)** — which are exactly the two seasons caveat 1 names, and +11.31
is caveat 2's largest single cell. **The whole statistic is a handful of squad flips in the GW26
column of two seasons.** Read the three below as one shape counted three times.

**1. Two of six seasons carry the whole signal.** The per-season schedule contrast is
+0.0023 / **+0.0277** / −0.0025 / **+0.0236** / −0.0074 / +0.0017 for 2020-21 … 2025-26 — positive
in **4 of 6**, with 2021-22 and 2023-24 supplying essentially all of it. Leave-one-season-out:

| dropped | mean | t |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2020-21 | 0.0086 | 1.21 |
| **2021-22** | **0.0036** | **0.67** |
| 2022-23 | 0.0096 | 1.41 |
| **2023-24** | **0.0044** | **0.72** |
| 2024-25 | 0.0106 | 1.69 |
| 2025-26 | 0.0088 | 1.23 |

Dropping either of two seasons **halves** the estimate. ⚠️ **Dropping BOTH reverses the sign:
−0.0015, t −0.65.** A first draft stopped at "halves", one step short of the step that makes this
bite. And **67% of the statistic is the GW26 column alone** — dropping it gives +0.0025, t 0.42.
This record has been caught once already reading a separating mean that was 94% one cell; this is
the same failure mode one level up.

**One reassurance, since its absence invites the question.** With settings {8,12,16,24} the ladder
slope puts most leverage on the endpoints, so the statistic could have been the `k=24` arm alone. It
is not: dropping `k=24` gives **+0.0104, t 1.11**, and dropping `k=12` gives +0.0081, t 1.36.

**2. The estimator is partly degenerate and heavy-tailed.** **18 of 108 arm-cells (17%) are exactly
zero**, and one arm-cell carries **11% to 21%** of its arm's total absolute effect (max |d| = 9.31,
9.62, 11.31). The GW26 column is a handful of large flips beside exact zeros: `k=24`'s six cells are
2.15, 11.31, −1.23, 6.46, **0**, 2.31.

⚠️ **Two corrections to how a first draft diagnosed this.** It said "on `HOLD` the intervention acts
once, at the opening build", which contradicts this document's own GW1 paragraph: there are **two
channels**, the opening fifteen and the weekly XI/captain re-pick, and a zero cell means neither
flipped. And it invoked the **triple-captain-preparation precedent to suppress the p — wrongly.**
That precedent bites because only 23 of 36 cells *placed the chip*, so the estimator bounded a flip
*rate* rather than a value. Here **every cell ran the intervention**; the zeros are measured
"changed nothing" outcomes, and the rule that applies is the opposite one — the `ChipCredit` 2×2
precedent, where dropping the zeros is what would be wrong. **Keep the p.** The precaution already
taken is the right one: aggregate to six season-level numbers before testing, which is what the
schedule statistic does.

**3. The late column is the noisiest, and the headline rests on it** — but ⚠️ **a first draft
quantified this against the wrong reference.** `sweep_inference.R`'s regime table gives `HOLD`
per-gameweek SE **0.466 early, 0.961 middle, 1.176 late**. Read as ratios to the early column
against a 1/√weeks null, *middle* is 1.75× over-length and *late* 1.67× — so late is not even the
worst on that measure. And 1/√weeks is a null nobody should have expected here: a squad flip is a
persistent offset over the whole window, whose per-gameweek variance does not shrink with length at
all. Most of the apparent excess is the **denominator** — the early column is quiet for a mechanism
reason (`k` never reaches the GW1 build) — so anchoring on it manufactures a ratio. **The
substantive point survives and the arithmetic does not:** per-column SD runs 0.14 at GW1 to 4.47 at
GW26, and the statistic rests on GW26.

## One thing the pre-registration said could not be done, which the data does in direction

⚠️ **This is post hoc. It was not registered, it does not resolve, and it is a hypothesis.**

The pre-registration said entry point moves the evidence regime and the scored calendar window
together, so a positive result could not separate "the constant wants to be a function of evidence"
from "of the calendar". The observed *shape* does separate them in direction, and I did not
anticipate that:

⚠️ **A first draft got the calendar reading's own prediction wrong.** It argued that a GW11 entrant
"contains all of GW16-28 and so should gain most". Containment is not the criterion — GW1, GW6 and
GW16 entrants contain all of it too. What varies is the window's **share of the scored horizon**,
`|[16,28] ∩ [g,38]| / (39−g)`: **0.34 / 0.39 / 0.46 / 0.57 / 0.44 / 0.23** for entries
1/6/11/16/21/26. Corrected:

- **The calendar reading** predicts **GW16 largest and GW26 smallest**.
- **The evidence reading** locates the benefit at high `n90`, where raising `k` pulls a data-rich
  estimate back toward the prior hardest, so it predicts **GW26 largest**.

Observed: **GW26 is the best column for all three arms** and GW16 is middling (+0.290, +0.855,
−0.304). So the conclusion survives — and for a **better** reason than the one first given: calendar
predicts GW26 to be the *minimum* and it is the maximum. **GW11 being the worst column is not the
calendar reading's prediction and must not be cited as refuting it.**

⚠️ **"Consistent across all three arms" is NOT corroboration here, and a first draft leaned on it.**
The three arms share the `k=8` baseline cell, so a shift in the baseline column moves all three
identically — which `schedule_screen.R` already says of the per-arm contrasts. The baseline's own
column profile carries the GW11 spike and the GW26 dip while the arms' profiles are smooth, so the
*shape of the difference* lives in one draw of the baseline. Re-baselining on `k=12` makes GW11 the
**best** column.

**The conclusion is not a baseline artifact, though the evidence first offered for it was.** The
baseline-free estimand — the per-cell ladder slope, which cancels the common baseline by
construction — reproduces the ordinal claim: by column it runs **+0.010, −0.011, −0.086, −0.020,
+0.076, +0.210**, minimum at GW11 and maximum at GW26. Per-column t on that estimand is −1.04 at
GW11 and +1.65 at GW26, and the profile is **non-monotone, which fits neither reading** — the
evidence reading predicts monotone in `g`. What discriminates is one endpoint, and it is the
endpoint carrying the whole statistic.

⚠️ **It also means the block's own recorded prediction — "raising k should help, and should help most
in the middle regime" — is not supported in the middle, on this grid.** But read that precisely: the
block meant the GW16-28 *calendar* window, and the entry-column middle is `{GW11, GW16}`, which is
not the same object. What is contradicted is the entry-column reading of that prediction. The
calendar reading is untested here and would need a within-season decomposition this harness does not
produce.

## What this buys

- **The gap is closed**: `BlendRateK` now has 36 banked cells per arm, and
  `stats/schedule_screen.R --committed` includes them.
- **The direction is registered and held**, which is worth more than the magnitude: the effect is
  the largest yet screened and still only half of what the design can see.
- **A sharper follow-up exists and is not this one.** The way to resolve a schedule is to make the
  comparison sharper, not to run more cells. Two candidates, in order: sweep `k` **below 8**, since
  the shipped value sits at the bottom of the range and the ladder's only clean feature is that 12
  is worse than both its neighbours; and decompose *within* a season rather than across entry
  points, which is the only thing that can separate the calendar and evidence readings.
