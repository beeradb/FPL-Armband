## 1. The recorded 112 was never season-scale, and this is the largest finding here

**Verdict: `refuted on arithmetic`, before any run.**

Every figure in the recorded floor table is a **12-cell total**. At `194c654` the sweep ran
`starts := []int{1, 11, 21}` over four hard-coded pairs, so each cell banks 38/28/18 gameweeks —
**336 cell-gameweeks** behind every number in it.

| recorded | 12-cell total | pts/gw | × 38 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 55 vs none | 112 | 0.333 | **12.7 a season** |
| 55 vs 45 (the "122-point spread") | 122 | 0.363 | **13.8 a season** |
| 55 vs 65 | 80 | 0.238 | **9.0 a season** |
| the baseline's own level (16182) | — | 48.2 | — |

**The strong form of the level check is the identical estimand on the identical grid.** This run's
baseline over *block E's own twelve cells* is **17264 points on the same 336 cell-gameweeks =
51.38 pts/gw**, against the recorded 16182/336 = **48.16**. Same quantity, same cells, **6.7%
apart** — where a four-cell reading would require the recorded figure to be 3.2× today's. That is
not a plausibility argument about what a sane `HOLD` rate looks like; it is a direct comparison.
(The weaker form still holds and is worth one line: 48.2 pts/gw is sane, where 4 cells would give
106.5 and 24 would give 26.4, so the candidate denominators are 2-3× apart and easy to tell apart.)

⚠️ **And it hands §3 a better argument than §3 first made: the baseline itself moved 1082 points
across those twelve cells between 2026-08-08 and now — 9.7× the 112 under dispute.** A paired
difference of 112 was never going to survive a tree that moved its own baseline by ten times it.

This is the standing **"convert per-gameweek × 38"** rule failing in the *other* direction — the
totals were never converted at all, and a 12-cell sum has been read as season-scale in `CLAUDE.md`,
`docs/notes/transfer-policy.md`, `docs/notes/scoring-model.md` and `TODO.md` for a week. Corrected
in all four on 2026-08-14.

⚠️ **The same edit corrected a second error**: the record described that table as "four-season
totals down a **single path**". It is three paths and twelve cells.

**Consequence for the item.** The contradiction it exists to settle is **0.333 pts/gw against
byte-identity, not 112 against zero** — an eighth the published size.

## 3. The recorded 112 does not survive on its own twelve cells

**Verdict: `refuted on the current data state`, on the pre-declared nested subgroup.**

Block E's grid is **nested inside** this one — 2022-23..2025-26 × starts {1, 11, 21}. Declared in
advance as a subgroup for exactly this reason, since it is the only apples-to-apples slice.

| arm | nested-12 total | cell-gameweeks | cells moved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| no floor | **+0** | 336 | **0 / 12** |
| floor=45 | **+0** | 336 | **0 / 12** |
| floor=65 | +25 | 336 | 1 / 12 |

Every difference is **identically zero**. The 336 also independently confirms §1's arithmetic.

⚠️ **This is a failed reproduction, not a refuted null, and the distinction is worth keeping.** A
first write-up invoked the standing rule's "a null can refute a recorded magnitude" exception.
Neither branch of that rule applies, because **no inference is involved**: the recorded quantity is a
deterministic total over twelve enumerated cells, and re-measuring the same estimand on the same
twelve cells today gives 0. Say "the recorded number does not reproduce on the current tree" — it is
strictly stronger, and it does not invite a reader to hunt for the mediator caveat that the
appeal-to-null framing then has to spend a paragraph disarming.

The mediator work does disarm it, and in the right order: **229.7 players a build are genuinely
removed, so the intervention ran and was declined.**

**It does not say the 2026-08-08 run was wrong when it was made** — the data state and five days of
code both moved, and this design cannot separate them. Attribution was deliberately not attempted:
restoring the data switches would not restore the code state, so that arm separates "data" from
"data plus a bundle".

⚠️ **Two other candidate causes were raised and both are closed, so the count is three, not two.**
(a) **`BankUpTo` differs between the runs** — `194c654` used `BankLimitFor(pair[1])`, the current
`sweepConfig` a flat `sweepBankLimit`. It cannot touch `HOLD`: the opening fifteen is built before
any transfer and `Hold` replays that squad. (b) **`194c654` read a hardcoded macOS config path and
discarded the error** (`cfg, _ := config.Load("/Users/bbowman/Projects/fpl/config.json")`), and
`config.Load` returns `Default()` alongside an error — the exact failure this project named two days
later when `loadConfig` replaced it. So block E may have run on `config.Default()`. **Closed**, by
diffing `config.json@194c654` against `DefaultWeights()@194c654`: every numeric scoring weight is
equal — horizon 5, bonus 1.5/0.5, `DefConCleanCoupling` 0.3, fixture 0.65, `MinutesHalfLife` 4,
`MinutesWeight` 1.25, bench 0.1, `BlendMinutesK` 5, `BlendRateK` 8, rest 2/0.83, and
`band_strength`/`prior_half_life`/`rate_half_life`/`set_piece_weight` all 0 on both sides;
`blank_run_penalty` is absent from the file, so the default applied either way. **It cannot explain
the discrepancy.** ⚠️ A review closed this on the ground that "`config.json` had no `review` key" —
**that is wrong**: the field is tagged `review_policy` and the file carries it. The correct ground is
the weights, since `Review` reaches only the transfer path and this is a `HOLD` result.

## 4. On points this is UNMEASURABLE, not unresolved — and the difference is arithmetic

| arm | mean pts/gw | × 38 | SE CR2 | df | seasons non-zero | t CR2 | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| no floor | −0.125 | −4.8 | 0.099 | 5.0 | **2 of 6** | −1.26 | — (capped at 1.58) |
| floor=45 | −0.024 | −0.9 | 0.024 | 5.0 | **1 of 6** | −1.00 | — (capped at 1.00) |
| floor=65 | +0.430 | +16.3 | 0.521 | 5.0 | 5 of 6 | +0.83 | 0.447 raw, 0.793 Holm |

**A degenerate difference vector is capped, not merely noisy, and that is the finding.** On balanced
cells the CR2 SE equals the between-season SE, so with `S` season clusters of which `k` are non-zero,

> **|t| ≤ √(k(S−1)/(S−k))** — 1.000 at k=1, 1.581 at k=2, 2.236 at k=3, 3.162 at k=4.

Against `t_crit(5) = 2.571`, **rejection is arithmetically impossible below four non-zero seasons.**
Verified two ways: the closed form, and by direct construction. `floor=45` has k=1 and its measured
t is **exactly −1.000** — the cap hit precisely, independent of effect size, which is the
`DefConCleanCoupling` shape verbatim. `no floor` has k=2 and −1.26 sits under its ceiling of 1.581.

Three consequences, and each retracts something this write-up first said.

- **⚠️ The pre-registered bar of 3 seasons was not conservative enough.** k=3 caps |t| at 2.236,
  still below 2.571. **On a six-season grid the bar is 4.** The registration was right in kind and
  wrong in level.
- **⚠️ Quote no threshold for `no floor` or `floor=45`.** `variance_components.R` prints 9.7/8.2 and
  2.3/1.8, and those are arithmetically correct **for an alternative the mechanism forbids** — an
  MDE is what a *uniform per-cell shift* would need to be seen, and a pool filter cannot produce
  one: it flips a fifteen or it does nothing. Quoting 9.7 invites exactly the wrong reading, that
  this comparison was sharp enough to see 10 points a season and saw nothing. Keep 50.9/31.0 for
  `floor=65`, whose 8 cells across 5 seasons is not a degenerate vector.
- **⚠️ The CI is uninformative by construction and should not be leaned on.** −0.125 ± 2.571 ×
  0.099 = [−0.380, +0.130] contains both 0 and −0.333, but **double the effect in those two cells
  and the mean, the season-mean spread and the SE all double, leaving t unchanged.** No alternative
  delivered through 2 of 6 seasons can ever be excluded by this interval. It is not that the test
  has low power; it has *zero* power, at any effect size.

⚠️ **"Equivalence test" is the wrong name** and the pre-registration used it too. An equivalence test
pre-specifies a margin and concludes equivalence when the CI falls *inside* it. This was a failure
to reject H₀: δ = −0.333 by CI inclusion. The instrument — testing against the recorded value rather
than against zero — was the right choice; the label was not.

**What would resolve it: only seasons in which the fifteen flips.** The clustered SE sees season
means alone, so **more start points cannot help this comparison at all**. At the observed rate of 2
flipping seasons in 6, reaching 4 needs of order twelve seasons, which the archive does not hold.
That is why the mediator is the primary result rather than a preliminary check — and it is a
statement about the instrument, not about the constant, which still has a right answer.

## 5. `floor=65` is the largest effect here and is not claimed

+0.430 pts/gw, **+16.3 a season** — and its own threshold is **50.9 / 31.0**, which it does not
approach. p Holm 0.79.

**The fragility is worse than the p conveys, and this is the argument to cite.** Its 8 moved cells
split **five up and three down** (a review said four and four; the cells say five and three), and
two GW26 cells — 13-week horizons, so tiny denominators — carry
**+11.23 and +9.85 pts/gw**. Together they contribute **+0.585 of the +0.430 mean, more than the
whole of it**: dropping them takes the arm to **−6.2 a season**. Leave-one-season-out spans +3.9 to
+26.5. That is the bench-slot-weights precedent word for word — "94% of the separating mean was one
cell of 36 and dropping it reversed the sign" — and it is worth more than the Holm p.

⚠️ **A first write-up said "the ladder is not monotone" and that is FALSE of this run.** On `HOLD`
the four arms read −0.125 → −0.024 → 0 → +0.430, **monotone increasing in the floor**, and the same
ordering holds on `hold_fixedcap` and `hold_nocap`. The non-monotone ladder is the *recorded*
`194c654` one. The error mattered in a specific direction: monotonicity across consecutive values is
one of the three structures this project accepts as establishing a constant, so read as structure
this ordering would argue for **raising** the floor above 55. It should not be, because three of the
four arms sit within 0.13 of each other separated by 1-2 cells apiece and the top rung reverses sign
when two cells are dropped — but **a correct verdict must not be reached by asserting a false fact.**

## 6. Two structural findings, neither about the floor

**(a) The near-theorem fails: the optimiser is not invariant to adding unusable players.** In
2021-22@11 the fifteen changes by **5 players** when the floor is lifted and **none of them is a
removed player** (`entered = 0`). The floorless pool is a strict superset, so the floored optimum
stays feasible; a difference means the DP-seeded local search settled somewhere else. This cell
carries −102 of the −113 raw total, so **81% of the primary contrast is a search artefact rather
than a floor effect**. Arm 45 was pre-registered as a check on the word "near" in that theorem, and
it found it.

⚠️ **81%, not the 90% a first write-up gave.** 90% is `102/113`, a ratio of **raw totals**; the
−0.125 pts/gw it was attached to is built from per-gameweek means, and this cell banks 28 gameweeks,
so its contribution is `−102/28/36 = −0.1012` of `−0.1247` = **81%**. Attaching a totals-share to a
per-gameweek figure is the same class of error as §1 — committed inside the write-up that corrects
§1, which is how easily this one recurs.

**(b) The pool-*nesting* extension is withdrawn.** `CLAUDE.md` read the reach run's byte-identical
8 cells as extending "to every setting **at or below 55 by pool nesting, not interpolation**". The
argument was: pools nest, so if the floorless pool returns the shipped fifteen then every
intermediate floor must too. **Its premise is false on 36 cells** — the floorless arm moves 2 — so
the conclusion does not carry, and `floor=45` independently moves 1 cell (2025-26@26, −11), the
same cell the floorless arm moves. Byte-identity at 8 cells was a fact about those 8 cells.

⚠️ Note what this does **not** show. A superset pool returning *worse realised `HOLD`* is not by
itself evidence of a non-optimal search: the optimiser maximises **its own objective**, not realised
points, and a genuinely better squad by that objective can score less.
⚠️ **This sentence said "maximises `XIValue`" and that is wrong — corrected when §7 measured it.**
`XIValue` (`swaps.go:78`) is the *transfer* search's scoring function and ignores the bench
entirely; `Optimize` climbs `xiValue + benchValue`, which is why §7 needed `Engine.ObjectiveFor`
rather than the exported `XIValue` sitting right there. The non-optimality claim in (a) rests
on `entered = 0` — a fifteen that contains no removed player was reachable in the smaller pool too —
not on the sign of the points.

**(c) The recorded table is block E's, not block J's.** At `194c654` **block J did not exist** —
only `E`, with six arms `{-1, 30, 45, 55, 65, 75}`, which is the only block that can have produced a
six-column table containing 30 and 75. Block `J` is a later four-arm re-judge in `TestDiagRejudge`.
Both are live today and both sweep `MinExpectedMinutes`. Reproducing the *recorded* table needs
`EXP=E -run TestDiagTransferPolicy`; re-judging it is `EXP=J -run TestDiagRejudge`.

⚠️ **The block letter does not identify the test.** `EXP=J -run TestDiagTransferPolicy` selects
nothing; the `blockPicker` guard failed it in 0.00s, exactly as designed. A `| tail` in the caller
swallowed that exit status and reported success — the "exit status you can trust" guard defeated
from outside. **Do not pipe a `scripts/replay` invocation.**

## 7. One discriminating build, and it acquits the shipped run — the defect is in the arm that does not ship

**Run 2026-08-14**, `probe-objective.log`, after adding `Engine.ObjectiveFor` and scoring both
squads on **the quantity `Optimize` actually maximises**. Not `XIValue`, which ignores the bench
entirely, and not `Squad.XIScore` or `ExpectedPoints`, neither of which carries the bench weighting —
any of those would report a search defect whenever the bench differs, which is most builds.

⚠️⚠️ **"0 of 36" is RETRACTED — the informative denominator is 1.** Found by code review, and it is
this record's own signature failure: **34 of the 36 cells cannot run the test at all.** With
`differs == 0` the two squads are the same *set* — `Optimize` keys `selected` by id, so fifteen
matching distinct ids is set equality — and the objectives are equal **by construction**, not by
measurement. A 35th, 2025-26@26, has `entered = 1`, so the guard correctly excludes it. **One cell
discriminates**, and pooling the rest manufactures a denominator that reads as 36 tests.

The probe now prints `canTest` and refuses the pooled figure; if `canTest` is ever zero it says
outright that the run establishes nothing about the shipped search.

**So the claim is: 1 of 1 discriminating build, and it acquitted the shipped run against one
specific alternative.** The condition that would convict is a floorless squad scoring higher with
`entered = 0` — such a squad is legal, affordable and in-pool under the floor, so the floored run
could have returned it and did not. In the one build where that could have shown up, it did not.

⚠️ **That is much weaker than the retracted sentence and it is the honest reading.** A genuine
search regression tomorrow would still print a clean result in all 34 structural-zero cells however
far from optimal the floored run were. **This counter is not a test of the shipped search's
optimality**; it is a test of *search monotonicity under pool widening*, one direction of which
happens to be informative.

The two moving cells split, and **neither is a shipped-config defect**:

| cell | obj floored | obj none | delta | entered | reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2021-22@11 | 60.8228 | 60.7429 | **−0.0799** | 0 | the **floorless** run wandered |
| 2025-26@26 | 58.6250 | 58.6294 | +0.0044 | 1 | the floor's **real cost** — uses a removed player |

**§6(a) is refined rather than overturned, and the direction is the opposite of what was expected.**
The search non-optimality is real — 2021-22@11 gives a **strictly larger pool a worse optimum**,
which an exact search cannot do — but it is the **floorless** arm that fails, not the shipped one.
**This half needs no denominator**: one cell where it happens convicts the search outright, so it
survives the retraction above intact.

⚠️ **What that licenses about the −102 is narrower than a first write-up said.** It does **not**
license "the −102 is the floorless arm's search failure" as a causal attribution: a 0.13% objective
difference does not *cause* 102 points. The sound and sufficient claim is that **the −102 is not
evidence about what the floor is worth**, because the two squads are objective-equivalent to one
part in eight hundred and the realised gap is a draw from squad-to-squad noise. Reading the causal
version would be the move this record forbids — treating "the point estimates moved apart" as
"the tie is gone". Yesterday's verdict holds and its main risk is retired.

⚠️ **An earlier reading of §6(a) said the reverse was the "materially more serious" possibility and
that it was untested. It is now tested and it does not occur.** Recorded because the wrong version
was stated aloud before it was measured.

**The magnitudes are the other finding, and they are small in a way that matters.** −0.0799 is
**0.13%** of a ~60-point objective, and +0.0044 is **0.007%**. Yet the first produced a **−102 point**
realised swing over 28 gameweeks. A difference of one part in eight hundred in what the optimiser
believes translates into 3.6 points a gameweek of what actually happened — which is the argmax
warning in its most concrete form here: **the objective barely discriminates between these squads,
so which one the search returns is close to arbitrary, and the realised gap is nearly all football.**

⚠️ **And the floor's entire measured value, in the only build where it binds, is 0.0044 objective
points.** That is not an argument for removing it — the record's rule is that a null is a tie — but
anyone re-opening this constant should know that is the size of the thing being argued over.
