# The per-arm thresholds are regenerated, and the reason they were lost is fixed, 2026-08-12

TODO.md carried **"Regenerate `stats/out/mde.csv`"** as a priority item: the file is gitignored
and was not on disk, and it holds the 23 per-arm detection thresholds behind the canonical
*median of 39 points a season*. Until it existed, the mechanical form of the
annotate-do-not-re-derive rule could not be followed at all.

The item described the work as **"a script over an existing CSV, not a sweep"**. ⚠️ **That was
wrong, and the way it was wrong is the point of this record.** The script was fine — but the CSV
it reads was gone *and so were its own inputs*. The sweep cells behind them survived only in
`/tmp`, on one disk, in a directory the operating system is entitled to empty. Nine sweeps were
one reboot away from being unreproducible without re-running the replay.

---

## 0. Terms used below

Following this project's rule that an identifier or an invented label carries a gloss on first
use. `HOLD`, `POLICY`, **cell** and **paired difference** are the standard four and are defined
in CLAUDE.md's "How to read the measurements"; the rest are local to this record.

| term | what it means here |
|---|---|
| **arm** (also *variant*) | one setting replayed across the whole grid — the alternative, or the shipped baseline it is subtracted from |
| **comparison** | one arm against the baseline, on **one** metric, under **one** estimator. The unit a detection threshold belongs to |
| **`variance_components.R`** | the R script that splits a sweep's paired differences into season and path variance, and prints what size of effect the design could have seen |
| **minimum detectable effect** | the smallest true effect the design would reliably catch. Written to `mde.csv`; the column quoted throughout here is `sig_season`, **the significance threshold in points a season** |
| **`scope`** | `mde.csv`'s first column: `arm` for a single comparison, `pooled` for a whole sweep's arms averaged. The per-arm rows are the honest unit and the pooled row is kept only so older figures stay readable |
| **season-clustered** | the primary estimator: treats each season as one independent unit. Three degrees of freedom on a four-season grid, and always conservative |
| **start-fixed** | the alternative estimator: treats the six entry gameweeks as fixed rather than sampled, giving fifteen degrees of freedom. Correct only where the between-season variance really is zero |
| **structurally inert arm** | an arm that *cannot* move a metric — a transfer setting leaves `HOLD` byte-identical — so its threshold reads exactly 0. Not a comparison that resolves nothing; a comparison that never ran |
| **provenance sidecar** | the `*.provenance.csv` a sweep writes *before* its first cell, declaring which arms it intends to run. It is how a killed sweep becomes arithmetic instead of a silently short cells file |

And the fourteen sweeps, which are the labels this record's tables are keyed on. Each name is the
directory the regenerated tables land in, not a name the codebase uses:

| label | grid | what its arms vary |
|---|---|---|
| **`minhl_frozen`** | 4 seasons | minutes half-life — how sharply the minutes model discounts older gameweeks. Arms: flat, 2, 8, 20 against the shipped 4 |
| **`vice4`**, **`vice6`** | 4 and 6 | the vice-captain fallback on or off — whether a blanking captain forfeits the bonus. The **positive control**: a mechanism-certain change |
| **`noise4`**, **`noise6`** | 4 and 6 | the **noise floor** — nudges of 1-2% built *not* to move anything, plus an identical-to-shipped control |
| **`teamform`** | 4 seasons | blending recent club form into the club rating, at weights 0.25 / 0.50 / 0.75 |
| **`hits`** | 4 seasons | the points threshold for taking a −4 hit: 1.5, 5.0, never, against the shipped 3.0 |
| **`anchored`** | 4 seasons | placing the chips on the calendar rather than at a fixed offset from entry, with 2, 4, 6 and full gameweeks of sight |
| **`oracleminutes`** | 4 seasons | hindsight about who plays: never-played, perfect lineups, perfect minutes |
| **`oraclearmband`** | 4 seasons | hindsight captaincy — the highest realised scorer in the eleven the model itself picked |
| **`oraclegate`** | 4 seasons | hindsight on the transfer gate — accepting or rejecting the model's own proposals on what they actually returned |
| **`oracleprices`**, **`pricetiming`** | 4 seasons | hindsight price timing — every transfer at the best price within two gameweeks either side. Two runs of the same comparison |
| **`oraclechip`** | 4 seasons | hindsight chip *timing* — the sweep axis the codebase calls `AxisChipWeek`, which places each scoring chip in its best week |
| **`teamnews`** | 6 seasons | FPL's own pre-deadline availability payloads in place of the replay's end-of-season reconstruction |

## 1. What was missing, and what has been done about it

| | |
|---|---|
| `stats/out/` | gitignored, absent from the worktree **and** the main checkout |
| per-sweep tables | all absent |
| sweep **cells** committed before today | four — `noise4`, `noise6`, `vice4`, `vice6` — plus two expected-goals-repair arms at snapshot `0104d9d` |
| sweep **cells** surviving only in `/tmp` | **ten** — `hits`, `teamform`, `oracleminutes`, `anchored`, `oraclearmband`, `oraclegate`, `oracleprices`, `oraclechip`, `pricetiming`, `teamnews` |

All ten are now committed under `cells/` here, with their provenance and means sidecars where
those existed.

**The fix is not to un-ignore `stats/out/`.** Those tables are derived, and a derived artefact in
git rots against the script that makes it. The fix is that **every cells file the pipeline reads
is committed**, so the tables are one command away:

```bash
stats/regenerate_mde.sh
```

Cells are the expensive half — one arm is minutes of replay — and the tables are seconds of R.
That asymmetry is the whole argument for which side of the line to commit.

⚠️ **`variance_components.R` writes one directory per sweep and never to bare `stats/out`**, and
the script preserves that. `stats/README.md` records why: a twelve-cell demo run once wrote to the
bare default and its figures were read as current by the accuracy snapshot for weeks, because the
output named no source. **So there is no `stats/out/mde.csv` and there should not be one** — the
TODO item's own filename was a description of the defect. The replacement is
`stats/out/mde-all-arms.csv`, which carries a `sweep` column so a figure can never again be
divorced from the comparison that produced it.

## 2. The regeneration

Fourteen sweeps, **264 `scope = "arm"` rows**, collected into `stats/out/mde-all-arms.csv` by
`stats/mde_aggregate.py`.

One sweep produced no tables, and it is **not a failure**. `oraclechip`'s two arms are
byte-identical on `POLICY` and on all three held rungs, so the response is identically zero and
R's linear-model fit (`lm`) has nothing to fit. That is the recorded `AxisChipWeek` result —
*"it moved nothing: +0.000 per gameweek on POLICY and all three held rungs in all 24 cells"* —
arriving from the opposite direction. The script reports it and continues, because this project's
rule is that a season producing identical output under an intervention is not a tie.

## 3. Two exact reproductions, which are the pipeline testing itself

Neither was aimed at. Both fell out of re-running the script on committed cells.

**The minutes half-life worked example reproduces to the digit.** These are thresholds in points
a season, season-clustered, each arm against the shipped half-life of 4:

| arm | `POLICY` recorded | regenerated | `HOLD` recorded | regenerated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| flat — no recency at all | 232 | **231.9** | 28 | **28.0** |
| half-life 2 | 80 | **80.0** | 24 | **23.5** |
| half-life 8 | 90 | **89.9** | 39 | **38.7** |
| half-life 20 | 133 | **133.0** | 39 | **38.9** |

**And the grid-width figures reproduce exactly.** These are the numbers the whole six-season
switch was argued on, measured on the vice-captain fallback as the positive control:

| | recorded | regenerated |
|---|---:|---:|
| `HOLD`, four seasons | 12.4 | **12.4** |
| `HOLD`, six seasons | 8.4 | **8.4** |
| `POLICY`, four seasons | 14.4 | **14.4** |
| `POLICY`, six seasons | 16.1 | **16.1** |

Both are worth more than the numbers themselves: they establish that the committed cells, the R
script and the recorded figures are one pipeline, so anything *else* that fails to reproduce is a
real discrepancy rather than version skew.

## 4. The 23-comparison population is recovered, with one caveat about how

Restricting to four-season grids, `HOLD` and `POLICY` only, dropping structurally inert arms, and
excluding the noise-floor and hindsight sweeps gives:

| | recorded | regenerated |
|---|---:|---:|
| comparisons | 23 | **23** |
| median, season-clustered | 39 | **39.6** |
| range across both estimators | 3.9 to 232 | **3.8 to 231.9** |
| median, start-fixed | 35 | 32.3 |

**The range settles a small mystery in how the recorded figure was quoted.** "Spanning 3.9 to
232" is not a range within one estimator: 231.9 is the season-clustered maximum and 3.8 is the
*start-fixed* minimum. The recorded span crosses both estimators while the two medians printed
beside it are per-estimator.

⚠️ **Read the n = 23 knowing the population was selected until the count matched.** Three
exclusions were applied and the third — dropping the hindsight sweeps — is what took the count
from 34 to exactly 23. That is an argmax over populations, the failure this project names most
often, and presenting it as independent confirmation would be dishonest. What makes it more than
a coincidence is that **the range then matched too, on a statistic not used to select the
population**, and that both exclusions are defensible on their own terms: the noise-floor arms
are built not to move, and the hindsight arms bound what *information* is worth rather than what
a *setting* is worth.

⚠️ **Two residual discrepancies, recorded rather than explained away.**

- **The start-fixed median regenerates at 32.3 against a recorded 35**, where the season-clustered
  median matched to 0.6. The candidate cause is that this reconstruction reads
  `stats/testdata/minutes_half_life_cells.csv` — a frozen fixture whose header has 23 columns
  where a current sweep writes 27 — while the original read that sweep's live cells, which are on
  disk in no form. **That is a hypothesis and nothing here tests it.** Re-running the minutes
  half-life block would.
- **The record says "three real sweeps" and this population spans five**: `hits`, `vice4`,
  `minhl_frozen`, `teamform`, `anchored`. Either the note miscounted, or the original 23 was a
  different set of comparisons sharing a count, a median and a range. The first is likelier and is
  **not established**.

## 5. What this does and does not license

**Does:** the 23 per-arm thresholds can be quoted and re-derived again, so the mechanical form of
the annotate-do-not-re-derive rule — *any comparison whose recorded threshold exceeds its recorded
effect, and whose threshold scaled by the measured ratio falls below it* — is computable.
`stats/out/mde-all-arms.csv` is its input.

**Does not:** none of this is a new measurement. Every figure here comes from cells replayed
before today, and it says nothing about whether those cells are what today's code would produce.

⚠️ **Specifically, `vice4` and `vice6` were replayed before `xgcrepair.go` existed** — the file
that reconstructs expected goals conceded for the four seasons the archive gives none. It was
added 2026-08-12; those cells are from 2026-08-11. So the reproduction in §3 confirms the
*pipeline*, not the *football*: the script still turns those cells into those numbers, which is
not a claim that a rerun today would produce those cells. The two sweeps that settle that are the
priority items above this one in TODO.md.
