`floor` is the within-season player-sampling SE of the season mean. An
`se` below its own floor cannot happen under independent seasons except

across the table, not at one row. ⚠️ Neither estimator dominates: `se`
generalises over FOOTBALL, `bootSE` over FOOTBALLERS. Their AGREEMENT is
what the verdict rests on, not either column alone.

`mde` is the difference this comparison would find 80% of the time. A
`diff` below it was never measurable, so a large p there is silence and
not agreement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FALSIFIED, on the omnibus: F(3,15) = 15.85, p = 6.44e-05; Friedman p = 0.0051.
The over-prediction the de-levelling removes is not common to all four
positions, so CLAUDE.md's shared-bias exemption does not cover it and
the argument written at the site does not hold. What the constant should
then be is a separate question and is NOT answered here.

Same bar on both columns, Holm over 6. A pair on ONE list
only is not a finding: the season-means column can read one cohort of
footballers 6 times over, and the bootstrap column holds the 6 seasons
fixed and so cannot speak to season-to-season disagreement at all.

The falsification also holds in the ADDITIVE parameterisation the
shared-bias precedent is actually about (table 4). Do NOT read an ordering
into the two tables' Holm counts — 2 and 1 of 6 — that is two sides of
an arbitrary line on the same rows, and orders nothing.
